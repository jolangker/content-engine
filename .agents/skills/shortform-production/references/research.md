# Research and script

Use this reference for research, factual claim revision, or script development. Read the selected board's editorial guidance for domain-specific rules; use the production brief when direction is supplied directly. Apply only the branches the production needs.

## Resolve research needs

Start after topic selection, from the selected episode's research needs and supplied sources. The discovery shortlist contains provisional editorial ideas; investigate only the selected topic rather than researching every candidate. Research may establish facts, provide context, or supply creative references. Record findings and their intended use in `SOURCES.md`, distinguishing evidence from creative inspiration. This stage is complete when each identified need is resolved or the brief reflects its remaining limitations. Skip it when no research is needed.

## Verify factual claims

In `SOURCES.md`, map each substantive factual claim to its supporting source URL, relevant evidence, and important qualifications. Choose sources suited to the claim: primary records for events and data, original studies for research findings, and authoritative references for definitions. Check that the source supports the actual scenario and wording. Use current sources when the answer could have changed.

For each claim:

- Separate documented facts, interpretation, and illustrative examples.
- Verify terminology, scope, and applicability to the episode's example.
- Preserve uncertainty and distinguish association from causation where relevant.

Claim verification is complete when every substantive factual claim has support or is narrowed to match the evidence. Record unresolved gaps and revise or omit unsupported claims. Keep fictional and illustrative material identifiable in its intended context.

## Select the hook before drafting

When the topic is locked and research is complete (or explicitly unnecessary), and the requested endpoint includes script development, develop exactly 3 distinct hook options before writing the script. Each option contains the actual opening line(s), a short explanation of its angle or audience appeal, and a promise the researched episode can fulfill. Vary the framing, not just synonyms; keep every factual implication within `SOURCES.md`. Recommend one with a brief reason. Present the options for human selection; delegated selection follows [presentation and durable records](invocation.md#presentation-and-durable-records).

Record the numbered options, recommendation, and pending choice in `EPISODE.md`. By default, set `stage: script`, `status: awaiting_review` with `next_action: choose hook`, present the options, and wait for the user's selection or supplied alternative. At this point `SCRIPT.md` has not been drafted. Topic approval and completed research do not select a hook; a script-only endpoint still includes this checkpoint. A research-only endpoint stops with the research deliverable and does not enter hook selection.

Once the user chooses, record the selected opening and its provenance in `EPISODE.md`, then draft the script around that hook. If the response does not identify a choice, resolve it before drafting. A previously selected hook or supplied final script is reused; do not reopen the choice unless a requested revision or changed evidence invalidates it. A materially new factual claim in a user-supplied hook needs verification before use.

Active automation or explicit delegation of hook selection replaces the human choice: record the 3 options and recommendation in `EPISODE.md`, select the strongest supported hook with a reason, and continue within the requested endpoint. The options remain internal unless requested; the selected hook can be included in a grouped progress update. Mark it as an agent choice under delegated authorization, not human approval. Follow [automation activation](invocation.md#activation): pending `after-hook`, `after-script`, and `after-storyboard` keep this human choice unless an earlier choice is separately delegated while preserving the activation checkpoint; user hook selection activates `after-hook` before script drafting. Permission to skip script review alone does not delegate hook selection.

## Develop the script

Use the selected hook and board or brief to determine structure, vocabulary, tone, pacing, and ending. Preserve the chosen opening and deliver its promise in the body and ending. Write the copy, dialogue, narration, or action required by the format. Assess the result against the intended audience experience and duration.

Keep narration or dialogue separate from exact on-screen copy when both are used. Label estimated timings and replace them with measured timings when available. Keep factual implications in visuals within the same evidence boundaries as the text.

The script is ready for review when it meets the brief's purpose, format, timing, and board-specific requirements, and any substantive factual claims map to supporting sources. Include source attribution in delivery copy when required by the board or brief. Preserve research links in the production.
