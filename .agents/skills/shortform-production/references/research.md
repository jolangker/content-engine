# Research and script

Use this reference when developing an episode's explanation or revising a factual claim. Apply the selected board's editorial framework; the patterns below serve evidence quality across boards.

## Verify the explanation

Start from the episode question and supplied sources. Use a light source check to assess a candidate angle, then research the selected explanation. Social posts can provide the audience situation or voice; verify factual claims independently.

In `SOURCES.md`, map the substantive claims to the supporting source URL, relevant finding or definition, and important qualification. Prefer original studies for research claims and authoritative professional references for established definitions; assess whether the source actually supports the scenario and wording. Note when material is a theory, a debated explanation, an association, or a causal finding. Use current sources when the answer could have changed.

For psychology and relationship material:

- Separate the observable situation, a person's interpretation, and a proposed explanation.
- Check the concept's definition and status before naming it. A familiar popular label may not be a recognized diagnostic or scientific term.
- Keep uncertainty in the narration when motives, personal history, or clinical status are not established. Match causal language to the evidence.
- Explain effects and responsibility clearly without assuming equal responsibility in every interaction.

If a concept does not fit, narrow the claim, propose another angle, or explain the experience in ordinary language with an appropriate basis. Insufficient support remains visible rather than being turned into a confident psychological label.

## Turn evidence into a script

Let the board determine narrative structure, vocabulary, tone, and the ending. Express one coherent message within the selected duration; choose the depth and number of examples for the material rather than imposing one account's formula on every board.

Keep narration separate from exact on-screen copy. Give estimated durations until measured audio exists, and name the source of actual timings afterward. A visual metaphor illustrates the explanation; it does not add unsupported facts or imply knowledge of a real person's internal state.

Before content review, verify that the script answers its opening question, its claims map to the research, and the ending follows from the explanation. Include concise source attribution in delivery copy when that copy is requested. Preserve source links in the production even when the final format has limited space.
