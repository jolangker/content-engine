# Story, motion, and sound — Di Balik Benda v6

Read this for topics, scripts, storyboards, animation, and sound. The visual invariants and measurements live in [creative direction](creative-direction.md). The source's motion and audio are **conceptual requirements**, not approved playback examples.

## Select a causal question

Topic territories include everyday mechanisms, motion/transmission, flow/control, and small design details that change behavior. Prefer several interacting subassemblies and linked state-dependent stages. Approximately **3–5 meaningful causal interactions** is a richness guide, not a raw-part quota. An explicit request for a simple object remains valid; preserve accuracy rather than inventing complexity.

Define the exact variant and one coherent cycle or subsystem explainable within the available duration. Compare shortlisted candidates using concise evidence and `strong / workable / weak` assessments:

| Criterion | Decision to establish |
| --- | --- |
| Causal depth | What changes the next assembly's behavior? |
| Visual discovery | What hidden relationship becomes clear through section, separation, or state comparison? |
| Motion diversity | Which different actions are physically supported? |
| Evidence | What establishes the selected variant's geometry, contact, timing, and operation? |
| Production scope | Can the necessary views, observation windows, and sound be made clearly? |
| Recognition / proximity | Which familiar encounter makes the object or behavior recognizable? If unfamiliar, how will the opening establish it? |
| Curiosity | What exact observable question challenges a plausible initial expectation? |
| Visual proof | Which sourced interaction and matched opening/ending views demonstrate the answer? |

A workable central question and visual proof are required; complexity cannot compensate for missing evidence. Record the selection reason. These assessments do not predict retention. Ease of implementation should not repeatedly reduce the subject to a single contact.

## Story invariants

1. **Central mystery:** start with a recognizable behavior and one concrete question answerable in the selected scope. A general “how it works” label is insufficient.
2. **First discovery and functional introduction:** supply a meaningful causal answer early. Introduce a component at its first explanatory use: show its function, then give its useful name. Roughly **8–12 seconds** is an adaptable first-discovery target, not a scientific threshold. Justify longer orientation when needed. A title, logo, moving camera, or isolated part name does not count as discovery.
3. **Linked discoveries:** map each question to its visual answer and the next causal dependency. Three to five questions is an optional starting range. Give useful answers throughout rather than withholding a simple answer for artificial suspense.
4. **Signature interaction:** choose one evidenced event for the clearest close inspection. Plan approach, contact/transfer, result, scale, observation needs, and sound. Reduce competing cues at the decisive instant. Its position follows causal order, not a fixed timestamp.
5. **Demonstrated ending:** match the opening behavior/input and a recognizable landmark, now showing the hidden cause. A section, aligned comparison, or purposeful replay must visibly answer the question. Merely returning to the assembled exterior is insufficient.

The [discovery panel](../assets/discovery-direction.png) illustrates opening → contact inspection → revealed answer. It is an independent conceptual study, not a complete storyboard or verified mechanism. A brief assembly overview or exploded view is useful when it resolves a spatial question; a front-loaded parts catalogue delays explanation.

Available narrative structures are follow the action, find the hidden part, compare verified states, and understand an evidenced failure. Choose and combine according to the mechanism; no fixed sequence of visual operations is mandatory.

## Source format and adaptable pacing

| Parameter | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 90 seconds | Source-format default; unless explicitly overridden, final timeline and export should match within one output frame |
| 9:16 vertical | Original film format; reference geometry is 1080 × 1920 |
| 14–18 explanatory shots, about 16 initially | Adaptable planning range, not a required count or ceiling; shots may belong to one continuous scene |
| 2–4 diagnostic poses for difficult actions | Useful planning guide, adapted to actual approach/contact/transfer/result; not a limit on animated intermediate states |
| At least two useful detail views when supported | Contact and internal-path inspection are examples; each must reveal new information |
| Frame rate | Keep the deliverable's selected rate; 30 fps was a source convention, not a creative lock |

More frames means richer explanatory shots and mechanism states, not higher fps. A new shot earns its time by revealing a component, state, contact, spatial relation, or consequence. Camera/title changes alone do not create explanatory depth.

This **editable example**, not a mandatory sequence, totals 90 seconds and 16 shots:

| Time | Job | Starting shots |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4 s | Recognizable behavior and central mystery | 1 |
| 4–12 s | First causal discovery and function-led component introduction | 2 |
| 12–35 s | Linked questions, new parts as needed, causal handoffs | 4 |
| 35–62 s | Signature interaction and its necessary dependencies | 5 |
| 62–80 s | Remaining consequences and coordinated operation | 2 |
| 80–90 s | Matched opening action and demonstrated answer | 2 |

Reallocate time according to explanatory difficulty. Measure narration against the timeline. A short recording calls for supported explanation or useful observation, not automatic contraction, empty repeated loops, or uniformly slowed motion. An explicitly requested static deliverable need not simulate 90 seconds, motion, or sound; state which source-format requirements do not apply.

## Voice and factual integrity

Use natural Indonesian, short concrete sentences, and accurate names introduced when needed. Pair terms with visible functions. Illustrative phrasing is **“Bagian ini menahan putaran. Namanya …”**, not a claim about any bundled mechanism. Avoid jargon without explanation, engineering-genius superlatives, sensational promises, and filler calls to action. Captions reflect spoken words; component annotations may add the names needed to read the illustration.

Research the exact variant before factual portrayal. Prefer manufacturer service diagrams, relevant patent embodiments, documented technical models, engineering education sources, and reputable teardowns. A patent establishes a described implementation; a teardown establishes the inspected model. Neither automatically establishes every object in a category. Trace important causal statements and hidden relationships to credible technical evidence; obtain a second independent source for ambiguous behavior.

Mark altered timing, non-scale spacing, displaced parts, removed shells, highlighted fluid, exaggerated clearance, and conceptual arrows. Simplification must preserve the relationship. Creative studies provide appearance, not technical evidence. When evidence is inadequate, narrow the explanation, select a documented variant, or retain a clearly conceptual study. Explain operation within the brief rather than adding unsupported repair or domain advice.

## Motion and attention

Map the supported chain: input → interacting assembly → affected assembly → output, with reset or alternative state only when real. Identify transfers, constraints, redirection, storage, release, or gating as applicable. Preserve a visible component/axis/path landmark and entry/exit state between shots.

Every narrated causal interaction must visibly show **input, contact or transfer, and response**. Design intermediate travel and contact rather than dissolving between before/after poses. Coupled components move concurrently when physically necessary; keep stationary housings stationary and rigid parts rigid. A visibility reveal changes inspection, not the mechanism's state.

Use **look here → action → response → confirmation** as a planning lens, not four required shots. Briefly cue the active component; show its action; pass emphasis to the affected part; synchronize the event sound and give the changed state readable observation time. The [interaction panel](../assets/interaction-direction.png) shows this attention language using conceptual shapes, not a real collision model.

Select isolate, axis-aligned separation, cutaway, selective transparency, macro, causal operation, state comparison, or whole-system return according to the question. A macro reveals contact, clearance, constraint, or transfer. An exploded view explains assembly relationships. A section retains the location and orientation of its enclosure.

Pan to follow a traveling part, push to inspect contact, pull back to relate assemblies, and use restrained illustrated layer parallax. Maintain orientation. Hold the camera at the decisive moment when helpful; separate annotation arrival, reframe, and the key event in time. A still observation hold is valid. No universal easing, transition effect, hold duration, or decorative ambient motion is specified.

Vary explanatory tasks, supported actions, and observation durations. Replays require a new purpose: exposed contact, a different verified state, or a slower marked inspection. Do not reset the assembly merely because the label changes.

## Interaction sound

Clear Indonesian narration has foreground priority. Every designed interaction event needs a synchronized cue **or a recorded reason for intentional silence**, such as masking or deliberate quiet contrast. Events include supported actuation, contact, engagement, release, causal handoff, and flow onset/change/stop. Coupled events may share a cue; sustained action may use one evolving sound. Music is optional and subordinate, and cannot replace interaction sound.

Distinguish material-aware physical mechanism sounds from softer **stylized editorial cues** for sections, separation, and attention. Do not imply a silent internal event was literally recorded. Shape onset to contact, body to travel/flow, and tail to release/stop. Vary by material and action rather than adding the same click to everything or a whoosh to each cut. Protect speech and leave quiet observation windows.

For animated work, record a compact cue sheet with event/component identifiers, action, onset/duration/stop, visible anchor, physical/editorial role, material and envelope, narration overlap/priority, shared cues or silence reasons, and asset provenance. Align onset to the visible event **within one output frame**, documenting intentional lead/lag. Review actual full-speed mixed playback and phone-speaker intelligibility. These tolerances are source implementation checks, not empirical engagement thresholds.

## Plan and review the actual deliverable

For an animation storyboard, record each shot's time and new question; visual answer and next dependency; first functional introduction; opening/discovery/signature/connection/ending role; entry/exit state and landmark; parts, action, and dependency; attention handoff and hold; sound event; 2.5D framing and camera purpose; visible bounds, motion sweep, caption clearance and any exception; diagnostic poses; rhythm; and technical evidence. Mark the first discovery, signature event, and opening/ending proof pair.

These are creative review criteria, not inherited human approval gates:

- Check all six story decisions: central mystery, functional first-use introduction, linked discoveries, signature interaction, demonstrated ending, and audience appeal alongside mechanism evidence.
- Watch approach/contact/transfer/result/reset when applicable, at normal speed as well as through diagnostic poses. Confirm the causal handoffs with sound off and labels temporarily hidden where feasible.
- Flag about **5 seconds** of narrated state change with no visible action, path evolution, or new state; justify a useful observation hold or revise. Flag **three or more shots** repeating framing and operation with only labels changing. Both numbers are review triggers, not absolute bans.
- Resolve repetition by exposing another required relationship, merging redundant shots, changing the explanatory view, or selecting a richer topic. Cosmetic text, color, dissolves, or zoom alone do not resolve repeated mechanical behavior.
- Compare the sound cue sheet to actual playback, verify event timing/coverage and speech clarity, and check the full moving silhouette against caption clearance. Static screenshots cannot verify synchronization or continuity.

Record limits honestly. If outcome analytics are later supplied, distinguish audience/traffic differences and missing metrics; an attractive replay may differ from a confusing one. Neither static board approval nor this creative framework proves retention gains. Approval and evidence scope are recorded in [provenance](provenance.md).
