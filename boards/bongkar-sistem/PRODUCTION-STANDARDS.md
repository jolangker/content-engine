# Production standards — Bongkar Sistem v2

These criteria apply to productions selecting v2. They supplement the production workflow, research, and HyperFrames technical contract. They do not start an episode or remove its checkpoints.

## Noticeable interactions

For every important step record actor, target, prior state, approach/contact, response, final state, camera attention, and sound cue. A subtitle, arrow, or part appearing already installed cannot replace the action.

As a framing starting point, let a detailed contact target span roughly a quarter of a 360-pixel phone frame or more. Approach or expose the mechanism when motion is too small to notice. Preserve physical scale; amplify schematic notation only when identified as such. Do not distort a valve's operating motion merely to make it larger.

Stage anticipation, action, consequence, and a readable result hold, often 0.4–0.8 seconds for a simple change; complex transfers may need longer. Settle the camera briefly on critical contact, then follow the consequence. Keep hands, captions, cuts, and fast camera motion from hiding it. Use compression, rebound, vibration, or splash only when motivated by the actual material/action.

Conditional examples: handle turns before the matching flow begins; cover approaches and meets its rim before the opening is closed; debris reaches and stays on the actual screen while water follows the valid route. Preserve meaningful process delays.

Review without captions at 360 × 640: what changed, what caused it, and where is the result? Watch the moving action as well as before/contact/result stills. If narration is required to locate the change, reframe or restage it.

## Full-screen environment

Build beyond the widest camera path. Ground, setting, subject, and depth continue beyond the edges. Appropriate houses, streets, vegetation, tools, utility lines, shadows, and material variation make the place inhabited. No visible floating pedestal or blank color moat. A local cutaway may expose ground or wall while the wider setting remains continuous.

Cropping the old slab larger is insufficient if a move reveals empty space behind it. Sky may be useful, but do not reserve half the screen as empty caption space. Density comes from contextual detail, not unrelated ornaments or mandatory ambient loops. Protect the focal action. Inspect wide, close, and path-extreme views for boundaries, clipping, and obstructed contacts.

## Pace and step density

Default: 90 seconds. Working range: 75–95 seconds. Every episode must be strictly above 60 seconds unless the user explicitly changes the floor; allow at least 65 seconds for a deliberately compact exception. Do not reduce the default just because six long scenes were easier.

For 90 seconds plan around 20–26 meaningful visual steps, most approximately 2.5–4.5 seconds, with short contact/discovery inserts and longer diagnostic/comedy moments as needed. A step is a new attempt, supported limitation, operation, transfer, consequence, adjustment, or result. New captions, different angles, rain, and replayed loops do not alone add steps.

Select a sufficiently rich researched system. Break a dense intervention into useful approach, installation, operation, and consequence where warranted. Do not invent components or failures. A continuous shot may contain several steps; one causal question may span several shots. Record steps and shots separately.

Editable nominal allocation: 0–4 hook; 4–22 early attempts/interventions; 22–48 further constraints/operations; 48–53 motivated comic development; 53–77 remaining steps; 77–86 whole-system result; 86–90 payoff. This is planning, not a finished script. Fit measured voice timing; do not just speed up the old narration.

Flag a shot longer than about five seconds with no second meaningful development. Add warranted action/observation or reallocate time. An arbitrary camera cut is not the repair.

## Camera inside the shot

For each shot record camera start/middle/end position, target, lens/view scale, action anchor, move purpose, and settle window. Use approach and later pull-back, subject/flow tracking, eye/low detail, overhead/section, and selected elevation/orbit moves when they reveal information.

As a first review target, a clear majority of principal shots should contain intentional camera travel or motivated lens changes. A diagnostic hold may stay fixed while the mechanism moves. More than two consecutive fixed shots needs an editorial reason. Cuts, subpixel drift, shaking, captions, or object movement do not prove camera travel.

A dolly changes position; a zoom changes focal length. Choose consciously and show useful approach/zoom-in and withdrawal/zoom-out rather than one ambient zoom everywhere. Use real perspective, occlusion, and parallax through 3D space. Preserve direction, component state, and connections. Settle on critical contact; avoid a dramatic flight through every action.

Verify camera transforms and rendered start/middle/end inside shot boundaries, then watch the movement. A collage of viewpoints cannot pass this test. Seek/keyframe diagnostics supplement moving visual review; canvas internals need rendered proof, not only DOM bounds. Do not interpolate across a deliberate hard cut or reset the camera on every caption.

## Discrete sound coverage

Plan narration, ambience, BGM, and discrete SFX separately. Rain is ambience, and a rain loop cannot count as installation, contact, opening, closing, flowing, or comic sound.

Give each focal audible event a source-appropriate cue and exact visible trigger. Layer approach/movement, brief contact, and resulting action/tail only where warranted. Examples: fitting movement → contact → material settle; handle friction/click → flow onset; cover lowering → rim contact → latch; chair movement → creak → reaction pause.

Cover every critical audible interaction or justify its intentional silence. Do not omit all planned accents because ambience exists or because the first asset search failed. Reuse/search/generate under the media workflow and authorized scope, or report the unresolved cue. Designed Foley is not a claim of documentary recording; avoid implying nonexistent mechanical properties.

Initial onset target: contact transient within about two 30-fps frames of the visible trigger, adjusted for physical distance and perception. Trim source silence. Focal SFX should rise clearly above ambience while preserving speech. Gain numbers do not prove subjective balance. BGM can support energy but cannot replace discrete cues.

Keep an event-to-cue ledger with source/hash, onset/end, track, gain/envelope, trigger, and export evidence. Review dry SFX against picture, then the voice mix. Check actual exported cue presence and synchronization. Nonzero waveform, whole-file decoding, ASR, or overall LUFS cannot certify audibility or comic timing. If perceptual listening is unavailable, report that limit and leave the subjective result unresolved.

## Completion evidence

| Area | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Pacing | Meaningful-step map and measured total above 60 seconds |
| Interaction | Phone-size before/contact/result and actual moving playback |
| Environment | Wide, close, and path-extreme full-context views |
| Camera | In-shot start/middle/end, camera-transform changes, and moving playback |
| Sound | Event-to-cue coverage, placement and export checks, explicit listening status |
| Overall | Whole audiovisual craft review separate from technical metrics |

Repair failed creative criteria or report a concrete remaining limitation. Do not label motion perceptually approved on the strength of stills, alignment, ASR, or runtime validation. Automation delegates gates only when explicitly selected; it does not remove these verification tasks.
