# Motion and audio direction — proposed v1

This static board establishes poses and intent. No animation, speech, music, or sound effects have been generated or perceptually verified.

## Camera follows the question

Establish the whole environment, follow the relevant part or material, approach the contact or exposed section, and return to the working whole. Pick the move by what the viewer must understand. A camera change alone is not a new explanation.

Keep a persistent coordinate system, component positions, and operating state. A pump stroke, load transfer, or fluid movement must remain consistent across camera views. Do not orbit simply to demonstrate 3D. Use a cut when changing viewpoint is clearer than a long flight.

The spatial vocabulary is: **establish, approach, open, trace, react, return**. Use any subset. Future episodes need readable intermediate states and varied explanatory jobs; repeating one camera push or one operating loop with different captions is insufficient.

## Acting and comic timing

The static character strip shows **confident plan → premature celebration → visible surprise → waiting/reset** as independent poses. These poses are not a mandatory chronological sequence.

Plant a prop or intent early, let apparent success register, interrupt it with a clear visual reversal, then hold the reaction briefly before the explanation resumes. An initial timing guide is roughly 0.4–0.8 seconds of anticipation, a 0.2–0.4-second reveal, and a 0.6–1.0-second reaction hold, fitted to the full 3–5-second comic development. These are proposed acting choices, not fixed production quotas.

An ending callback recalls a specific earlier action, prop, or expectation. Resolve the useful explanation first. Avoid a gag that makes the completed system appear to fail unless that limitation is the actual lesson.

## Sound concept

Use clear conversational Indonesian narration with a composed delivery. Let the visual character be more excitable than the narrator. Silence or a brief music dip can give the audience room to notice the comic reveal. Avoid a laugh track or a loud impact for every joke.

Use source-appropriate mechanical/environmental sound when it helps read action: wheel contact, a small latch, air movement, foot movement, a chair unfolding. A short suitcase-wheel or wing cue can support the fictional visitor gag. These are cue proposals; no library or specific track is selected.

Music is optional and decided per episode. A light, unobtrusive rhythmic bed can support building momentum, with space around explanation and payoff. Preserve speech intelligibility. Do not equate constant music with pacing.

For an actual narration request, follow the project audio guide: explicit ElevenLabs provider, root `.env` default voice, `eleven_v4`, appropriate delivery tags, validation, and fingerprinted reuse. This board does not choose a voice ID, authorize synthesis, or replace those rules.
