# Editorial system — Bongkar Sistem v1

## Promise and audience experience

**“Setiap bagian ada alasannya.”** The viewer should finish able to connect a component to the problem it solves and explain how the parts work together. The viewer also follows a recurring builder whose confidence is often one step ahead of the system.

Use Indonesian for audience-facing language. Address the viewer as `kamu`; keep the narration conversational, curious, concrete, and composed. The initial audience is general Indonesian viewers who enjoy mechanisms, infrastructure, and practical science. The name, promise, and detailed rules here are proposed implementation of the selected concept, pending package review.

## Scope and candidate pillars

| Pillar | Story opportunity | Illustrative topic seeds, not researched episode selections |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Systems around the house | A desired comfort meets several interacting constraints | Rainwater storage, ventilation, water supply, drainage |
| Infrastructure under our feet | Expose layers that distribute, support, carry, or protect | Railway beds, road drainage, buried utilities |
| Machines as a working chain | Several parts solve successive problems in one coherent operation | Lifting, pumping, braking, material transport |
| Everyday technology at a useful scale | Follow a resource or action across connected components | Cooling loops, filtration, distribution networks |

Prefer a coherent subsystem with several meaningful causal relationships. Judge a topic by causal depth, visual discovery, potential for environmental continuity, source availability, and production scope. Part count alone does not make a rich story. Avoid defaulting to a shallow object simply because its asset is easy to draw.

## Story engine

1. Start with a visible goal or an intentionally incomplete attempt: `Kalau kamu…`.
2. Let its actual limitation become visible: `Tapi…`.
3. Add or change something for an evidence-backed reason: `Jadi…`.
4. Show what the intervention changes and what limitation remains.
5. Repeat only as the selected system requires.
6. Show the working whole and answer the initial goal.
7. Pay off a planted visual joke when it strengthens the ending.

The sequence is explanatory reconstruction, not a claim that the technology was historically invented in this order. The script may use roughly four to seven causal developments as a first planning pass, but this is adaptable. A source-supported story can need fewer or more. Each new beat must add information.

Do not manufacture a failure or add an unnecessary component to satisfy `tapi/jadi`. Mix conjunctions naturally and give the audience moments to observe. Introduce a technical term after the viewer has seen the part's need or function. Resolve any scale changes, hidden sections, or schematic notation before they become ambiguous.

## Duration and pacing

The accepted default is **90 seconds**, with simpler topics allowed to finish at **60–75 seconds**. This is a narrative target, not a requirement to stretch a short explanation. Record an episode override explicitly and measure the final narration and export.

| Nominal 90-second window | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| 0–5 seconds | Concrete hook and first attempt |
| 5–70 seconds | Build the system through problems and solutions; place a 3–5-second comic development where motivated |
| 70–85 seconds | Let the audience observe the completed causal chain and resolve the opening |
| 85–90 seconds | Brief callback or payoff when supported; otherwise use a satisfying system result |

The comedy beat is inside the 90 seconds. Some visual humor can overlap narration, while a punchline needs a short listening/looking window. Do not insert a spoken fact at the instant the audience must decode a visual joke. No fixed scene count is inherited from another board. Distinguish camera views from causal beats; a continuous environment can contain several views and state changes.

## Situational comedy

The recurring builder is practical, eager, and prematurely satisfied. The audience laughs at misplaced confidence and recognizable behavior. Keep the builder sympathetic and capable of learning.

- Middle: setup, apparent success, small reversal, useful next intervention. A first planning allowance is one principal 3–5-second comic beat.
- Ending: return to an action or prop planted earlier. The explanation resolves before the final comic reward.
- The narrator stays relatively calm. The character's timing, pose, and prop supply most of the joke.
- Support physical readability with anticipation, a clear reaction pose, and a short hold. Do not cover critical contacts or flow with acting.
- Scale exaggeration and theatrical visitors are permissible comic conventions. Keep them visibly separate from technical diagrams and measured quantities.
- Never change the real mechanism, invent a failure, or imply that a deliberately silly experiment is a validated installation for a joke.
- Humor is not mandatory at every step. A serious limitation can be explained plainly.

### Fictional example: visitors with luggage

The builder carries a deck chair into the scene early. When the room appears comfortable, the builder prepares to rest. An oversized mosquito arrives with a tiny suitcase; two smaller travellers follow. Illustrative line: `“Lho, kok ada yang ikut pindahan?”`

In a researched ventilation episode this could introduce an intake-screen discussion only if the identified system and evidence support it. In these style studies it is a visual joke, not a validated ventilation design or biological-scale diagram. A later callback could show the builder finally relaxing while visitors wait outside the appropriate barrier. The payoff must not imply a technical result that has not been established.

## Narration and text

Use concrete verbs and brief clauses. Explain one new relationship at a time. Useful illustrative wording includes `“Kelihatannya sudah beres.”`, `“Tapi bebannya belum ke mana-mana.”`, and `“Jadi, bagian ini kita tambahkan.”` These are writing examples, not verified claims or a finished episode script.

Caption the spoken words accurately. Break captions by meaning, usually two to six words per visible group and at most two lines; fit the actual speech and platform. Scientific terms may require a longer group. Keep labels anchored to the relevant component and introduce names after function. Use numbers only when they have a source and necessary context.

No default spoken CTA is required. A suitable episode can close with a concise result and a visual callback. Account identity and channel-specific publishing copy remain unresolved until a production selects a destination.

## Research and interpretation

- Use the production research workflow and cite primary technical documentation, credible engineering references, or appropriate research for factual claims. Treat creative reference videos as leads, not evidence.
- Map each consequential problem, proposed fix, operating claim, number, and quantitative comparison to a source. Record operating conditions, scale, uncertainty, and meaningful exceptions.
- Verify the selected real implementation before using a schematic. Preserve causal order, continuity of state, contact, flow direction, and what is hidden or exposed.
- Distinguish a thermal concept, an illustrative air path, and a complete installed ventilation system. Phrases such as `tanpa listrik`, exact burial depth, and guaranteed temperature reductions require evidence and stated conditions.
- Avoid ungrounded universals about loads, stopping distances, resource yields, or geological processes. Attribute estimates and explain the conditions that make them applicable.
- Distinguish present-day explanation from actual history. A humorous reconstruction does not establish historical priority.
- Write research and project documents in English; scripts, captions, and narration remain Indonesian unless an episode explicitly chooses another language.

## Episode review questions

Can a viewer explain why each important part exists? Does every new view teach something? Does the same world retain its state across views? Is the comic interruption motivated and legible? Can the science be understood without the joke? Does the final whole answer the opening? Are all substantive claims traceable? Test those questions during an actual production; a static board cannot establish animation or audio quality.
