# A world you can open

The system lives in a recognizable miniature environment. Daylight, solid materials, generous shadows, and visible earth layers establish spatial relationships. The scene carries the explanation; text supports it. Color tokens for 3D materials are albedo inputs in sRGB, not a promise that every lit pixel matches the input hex. HTML typography and caption colors use the exact tokens.

## Composition

Use an establishing world view, useful sectional views, diagnostic close-ups, and whole-system returns. Keep a meaningful action or causal relationship dominant in each view. Environmental depth can come from trees, a nearby building, terrain, and the functional system itself; arbitrary decorative overlays are unnecessary.

The new static samples are actual original Blender 3D renders with editable geometry. They demonstrate still-image art direction. Future productions may use true 3D, 2.5D, or a technical section as required by camera, occlusion, and explanatory needs. Do not replace a promised spatial move with a still-image zoom and represent the result as equivalent 3D motion.

Treat the stated safe area as an initial vertical-feed working region. Validate against the actual destination UI. Full-bleed scenery may extend behind platform controls; essential text, contacts, and punchlines must remain visible. Native samples include small review identifiers outside this safe area; those are review labels, not a production text standard.

## Captions and labels

Place the speech rail in available sky or a calm surface near the current action. In these studies it is high in the frame to protect both the cutaway and character. A production can relocate it intentionally when framing changes; avoid distracting drift during a spoken phrase. Keep its meaning close to the visual without covering the new mechanism or joke.

Cream caption backing with ink type provides stable contrast. Use warm or teal emphasis as graphic marks rather than small low-contrast text. Label only necessary parts, anchored to real geometry. No title paragraph, fixed large header, data card, or floating dashboard is required inside an episode. The board's large editorial titles describe the identity and are not mandatory video overlays.

## Material and notation rules

Turf, layered earth, terracotta roofs, cream walls, teal human props, restrained metal, and occasional yellow focal props form the initial palette. Natural material variation is allowed within these roles. Preserve character colors between scenes.

Warm orange and cool blue may show temperature within a thermal episode. They must not silently change meaning to pressure, danger, or flow direction in the same episode. Airflow beads are schematic notation; actual fluid appearance and physical scale require a topic-specific decision. Define a color legend when the distinction is not otherwise clear.

An exposed section is a viewing convention. Make the opening/cut plane and the visible route readable, preserve connected components, and make any magnification explicit. Avoid decoration that looks like a real pipe, wire, force arrow, dimension, or sensor.

## Character and humor

One small builder in yellow/teal carries recurring confidence and reactions. Preserve silhouette, cap, pocket, and boots. Build readable poses before adding detail. The character must be large enough to read during the gag but may disappear during a complex explanation. No continuous talking avatar is required.

The mosquito travellers in the comedy study are overt theatrical exaggeration. They establish timing and prop possibilities, not a new mandatory mascot or measured biological scale. Fictional comic props cannot introduce an unsupported technical claim.

## Timing and scope

Use the accepted 90-second default and adaptable shorter exception in EDITORIAL.md. Shot count is not fixed. Hold critical transfers long enough to observe them; let setup, reaction, and payoff have separate readable moments. Motion vocabulary and audio remain conceptual in this static package. See MOTION-AND-AUDIO.md.
