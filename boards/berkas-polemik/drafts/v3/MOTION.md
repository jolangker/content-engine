# Berkas Polemik — richer motion proposal

Status: revised conceptual proposal following the `penutup-proklamasi` test. The user requested richer motion and subsequently rejected the monotonous slide transitions. The approximately 90-second / 16-primary-scene format remains confirmed. A completed test production exists; this board revision changes guidance only and does not alter or re-render that production.

## Test feedback and controlling transition rule

User feedback: “gua kurang suka transisi antar frame itu slide. monoton banget”. Inspection of the test's [root transition source](../../../../productions/penutup-proklamasi/video/index.html) finds ten `push-slide` boundaries and five crossfades across fifteen scene changes. The first five boundaries all push left, each using the same 0.5-second timing. This is a source inspection, not a new perceptual review of the exported video.

**Do not use full-scene slide/push transitions for this refinement.** This user preference overrides the previous directional-handoff allocation and any generic style preset recommending slide as a primary transition. Do not substitute alternating left/right slides, vertical pushes, or full-page wipes that preserve the same carousel feel.

Movement of an individual document, loupe, or diagram element *inside* a scene remains available when it explains the content. Keep that distinct from moving the complete outgoing and incoming canvases across the screen.

The [previous motion proposal](revisions/motion-before-transition-feedback.md) is retained to explain the tested treatment. The prohibition reflects user feedback; the replacement choices below remain a proposed implementation vocabulary, not an approved new render.

## Direction

Treat the film as a moving archival workspace. The viewer travels between objects, inspects a detail, and watches the relationships between sources take shape. Use spatial continuity, layered objects, and purposeful transformations to create richness while keeping frame copy sparse.

The desired increase in motion richness is confirmed. The specific choreography and allocations below are proposals, not previously approved animation.

## Three coordinated layers

1. **Spatial staging:** paper, photographs, the desk plane, and editorial annotations occupy distinct visual depths. A short move can bring one source forward while another recedes. Keep essential source text stable during reading; do not apply a constant zoom to every scene.
2. **Explanatory action:** show the change the narration describes. Align two source details, move a loupe to a word, draw an editorial circle around a specific discrepancy, or extend a connection between two identified records. Each line must have named start/end objects and a communicative job.
3. **Scene handoff:** select the connection from the actual relationship between scenes. Preserve a meaningful detail's position for a match cut, continue a camera move into the same source, or cut directly when the argument changes. A highlighted circle can become the next scene's loupe at the same anchor. The transition belongs inside the existing scene timeline, not after the 90-second duration.

Default per scene: two meaningful internal actions, one optional secondary response, and a settled reading/result pose. These develop the same primary composition; they do not increase the count beyond sixteen scenes.

## Proposed movement vocabulary

| Treatment | Viewer sees | When it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Folder and paper staging | A folder opens; sheets fan out and one is pulled forward, with restrained tilt and changing overlap. | Introducing a source trail or selecting evidence. |
| Comparison alignment | Separate documents slide into alignment at the relevant mark, word, or date; a difference becomes visible. | Comparing evidence without a paragraph of explanation. |
| Detail inspection | The view travels from the whole source to a selected detail; a loupe or crop enlarges it and then holds. | Reading an actual source detail with context retained. |
| Drawn annotation | An editorial underline, circle, or source-to-source connection is drawn in sync with the narrated finding. | Directing attention or explaining a documented relationship. |
| Depth reveal | A front object moves aside to reveal a corroborating object behind it. | Adding context; do not imply the source was historically hidden. |
| Visual continuity | A circle, document edge, or anchor position is preserved across a scene boundary. | Making the sequence feel connected rather than like independent cards. |

The physical styling remains firm and tactile. Use brief overshoot only where material weight warrants it; avoid elastic bounce as the account's default. Motion should vary in direction, speed, and composition, not simply repeat the same entrance sixteen times.

## Rhythm and intensity

- Brief accents: approximately 0.2–0.35 seconds.
- Main object actions: approximately 0.45–0.9 seconds.
- Deliberate spatial inspection: approximately 0.8–1.4 seconds.
- Settled evidence/result hold: typically 1.2–2 seconds, longer when the actual excerpt requires it.
- Use the boldest motion at the opening, the key comparison, and the principal finding. Let contextual and concluding scenes breathe.
- Do not run two competing focal actions simultaneously. Supporting depth or shadow changes may accompany the primary move; essential text stays readable.
- Use a coherent transition vocabulary, chosen by narrative and visual relationship rather than a numerical quota. Full-scene slides/pushes are excluded. Do not replace the old slide-heavy allocation with another repeated preset or an arbitrary new effect at every boundary.

## Replacement transition vocabulary

| Boundary relationship | Proposed treatment | Required visible connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A new idea or decisive statement | Direct cut, timed to narration or a visual action. | Deliberate contrast of composition or emphasis; no travel across an empty canvas. |
| The same object/detail continues | Match cut on position, scale, shape, or a shared word. | Name the outgoing and incoming anchor and preserve their geometry at the boundary. |
| Whole source to the detail being inspected | Continuous camera move into the selected region, carrying the view into the next composition. | The same recognizable source/detail remains traceable; no zoom out and reset at every scene. |
| A specific object opens a new view | A local mask opens from the loupe, paper aperture, or another meaningful shape. | The revealing object performs the action; do not move a full rectangular canvas sideways. |
| A context or temporal shift needs gentleness | Brief dissolve, used selectively. | The change in narrative register justifies the blend. |

Before authoring, annotate all fifteen boundaries with the two scene IDs, their relationship, the selected treatment, the visible anchor if applicable, and a short reason. Inspect several consecutive boundaries together: changing a preset's name, direction, or duration does not establish perceptual variety. Keep richer choreography inside scenes while letting some boundaries be simple cuts.

## Six-second comparison example

| Local time | Choreography |
| --- | --- |
| 0.0–0.7 s | The paper stack opens, introducing the physical source objects. |
| 0.7–2.2 s | The second document slides into alignment at the comparable detail. |
| 2.2–3.8 s | Attention moves to the missing counterpart; an editorial outline traces the precise location. |
| 3.8–5.4 s | The comparison settles so the viewer can see the difference. |
| 5.4–6.0 s | The highlighted shape hands off to the next scene's inspection view. |

This is a motion study proposal based on simulated source material. A missing mark does not establish forgery or intent. No historical signature or wording is animated as though it were being authored or erased.

## Sound and implementation boundary

Support selected actions with a paper movement, pencil trace, restrained mechanical click, or brief low impact. Avoid a sound on every movement. Narration leads, with quieter reading holds. These remain audio directions, not generated assets.

Before implementation, search the current registry for the selected named moves, follow the camera/keyframe and animation contracts, and verify the actual rendered motion and reading holds. This document specifies intent; it is not evidence that any animation has been built or validated. Existing static exports remain static identity studies.
