# Bukan, Tapi — editorial framework v1

Status: approved by the user on 2026-11-23.

## Purpose and audience

Narrated parables for a general Indonesian-speaking audience. The viewer thinks they already know how it ends, is turned around, and leaves with one line worth quoting.

## Scope

Original stories about money, work, relationships and ways of thinking, 75–110 seconds, told in Indonesian. Characters are nameless archetypes (a carpenter, a trader, a father, a neighbour), or a named everyman when the story needs one. Stories, characters and wording are original; reference episodes are never retold. A story that states a fact about the world (a figure, a study, an animal's behaviour) follows the production research rules; a pure parable needs no source and must not be presented as a true event.

## Story shape

| Beat | Share of runtime | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | first 8–10 s | No greeting, no title. The first sentence puts two parties or one odd premise on screen. |
| Setup | to about 20% | The rules of the test: same order, same money, same question. |
| Path A / round 1 | about 20–40% | The first way of doing it, shown with a visible count or result. |
| Path B / round 2 | about 40–60% | The second way, comparable shot for shot. A single-character story presses the same question harder instead. |
| False expectation | just before the reversal | The obvious guess is said aloud, by the narrator ("siapa pun pasti mengira…") or a character. |
| Reversal | starts at 65–80% | The outcome is shown, then explained in one or two sentences. |
| Closing maxim | last 10–13 s | One contrastive line, "bukan X, tapi Y" or an equivalent. No call to action. A picture-only last beat is allowed. |

## Duration and pace

75–110 seconds. Narration runs at about 2.5 words per second (190–270 words) with almost no pauses. Shots follow clauses: one new picture or pose per clause.

## Voice

One calm, warm adult male narrator in everyday Indonesian ("kamu", not "Anda"; no slang that dates quickly). Character lines are read by the same narrator and appear as speech bubbles. The voice itself is not chosen yet; see [AUDIO.json](AUDIO.json).

## Worked example (illustrative, not a script)

"Dua tukang kayu": two carpenters get the same order of 100 planks and one day. The first saws without stopping; the second stops every hour to sharpen. "Siapa pun pasti mengira yang tak pernah berhenti akan menang." The one who kept stopping finishes first. Closing: "bukan yang paling lama bekerja, tapi yang paling tajam alatnya." Plank counts in the samples are invented for the picture.
