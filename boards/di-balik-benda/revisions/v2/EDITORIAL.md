## Purpose and audience

Explain how familiar objects and small systems work for curious Indonesian-speaking general audiences. Assume no engineering background. Technical viewers should recognize the mechanism; first-time viewers should be able to follow its input, interaction, and output.

Board identity: **Di Balik Benda**. The host's role is a clear, curious guide who opens things up. The account handle, bio, platform, and publishing cadence remain undecided.

## Scope and pillars

- Everyday mechanisms: closures, pumps, switches, click mechanisms, latches.
- Motion and transmission: gears, levers, springs, bearings, and linkages in a selected real application.
- Flow and control: simple valves, dispensing systems, and pressure-driven devices.
- Useful design details: how one small geometry or material choice changes behavior.

These are topic territories, not verified claims or a required release sequence. Do not inherit relationship psychology or political editorial assumptions from the workspace's other boards.

## Reusable content concept

Start from a visible behavior or a precise question, reveal the hidden component that makes it possible, and show the causal chain. The viewer should see at least one relation that ordinary exterior footage hides.

Available structures:

1. **Follow the action:** input → contact or transfer → output. Good for one compact mechanism.
2. **Find the hidden part:** familiar whole → isolate a component → reveal its role → return to context.
3. **Compare two states:** idle versus operating, open versus closed, or two verified implementations.
4. **Understand a failure:** normal operation → specific failure → visible consequence. Use only when supported by sources and appropriate to the topic.

The visual explanation determines the structure. Default format: a **90-second vertical explanation of one mechanism**, with a richer sequence of explanatory shots. A current explicit episode instruction may override this default; inherited older episode timing does not override it for a new production.

## Duration and breakdown depth

Plan **14–18 distinct explanatory shots or storyboard frames**, with roughly 16 as a starting point. This is a planning range, not a fixed scene quota or an upper limit. Shots may belong to the same continuous scene. An added shot must reveal a component, state, contact, spatial relationship, or causal consequence; changing a title or camera angle alone does not add explanatory depth.

Use the extra time to inspect anatomy, explain the critical interaction, and trace the operating cycle. Prioritize the interaction that makes the mechanism work. Include at least two useful detail views when the subject supports them, such as the contact surface and the path through an internal assembly. Pick 2D, 2.5D, 3D, or a mixture by explanatory need.

For each critical action, storyboard its relevant intermediate states: initial state, applied input, approach or contact, transfer or deformation, resulting state, and release or reset when the actual mechanism has one. Show 2–4 diagnostic poses for a complex interaction so the motion between the before and after is designed. These poses may be subframes inside a shot; the 14–18-shot range is separate from diagnostic poses. Do not invent mechanical stages to fill a template.

An illustrative allocation totals 90 seconds:

| Time | Explanatory job | Starting shot allocation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–7 s | Familiar whole and the central question | 1 |
| 7–22 s | Separate and identify relevant parts | 3 |
| 22–37 s | Trace input toward the active contact | 3 |
| 37–62 s | Inspect and animate the critical interaction in detail | 5 |
| 62–77 s | Follow the result; show release, reset, or another relevant state | 2 |
| 77–90 s | Reassemble or return to context and demonstrate the whole cycle | 2 |

This is an editable pacing example, not a mandatory hook, sequence, shot count, or ending. Reallocate time where the researched mechanism needs it. Avoid a long introduction that consumes the breakdown budget.

Write and measure narration against the 90-second timeline. If the recording is shorter, expand the supported explanation or add justified observation time with meaningful state changes. Do not automatically shrink the composition to the recording length, pad it with an unchanged loop, or slow the whole film merely to hit duration. Check the final timeline and export against 90 seconds to within one output frame, unless the user explicitly chooses a different duration.

Here, "more frames" means more explanatory shots and intermediate mechanism states. It does not request a higher frame rate. Use the production's selected frame rate; a new production may retain the existing 30 fps convention unless its brief says otherwise.

## Voice and language

Use natural Indonesian, short concrete sentences, and accurate names introduced only when needed. Pair a technical term with a visible function. Prefer “bagian ini menekan...” over abstract claims about engineering genius. Avoid sensational language, unsupported superlatives, unexplained jargon, and filler CTAs.

Illustrative prompts only, not verified scripts:

- “Apa yang terjadi di dalam kepala semprotan saat ditekan?”
- “Bagaimana gigi resleting bisa saling mengait?”
- “Bagian mana yang meneruskan gerakan?”

## Visual storytelling rules

- Animate the actual relationship being explained: contact, constraint, transfer, deformation, or flow.
- Preserve the identity and location of the active part through a zoom or cut. Reorient intentionally and provide a recognizable landmark.
- Reveal hidden surfaces using cutaway, transparency, separation, or a change of viewpoint. Pick whichever makes the mechanism clearest.
- Use functional labels and arrows only when they resolve a question. Distinguish movement from force and fluid flow.
- Show one meaningful causal relation at a time. A part can remain still while narration explains it.
- If using a cross-section, mark which shell is removed; if separating parts, make clear that the spacing is explanatory.
- Captions contain spoken words; part annotations may add concise names needed to read the diagram.
- The storyboard must list what each shot newly reveals, its time range, relevant parts and contact, selected medium, camera purpose, and intermediate poses. Keep a stable component landmark between whole, exploded, sectional, and macro views.
- During verification, inspect the approach, contact, transfer, result, and reset where applicable. A static before/after pair does not establish that a breakdown animation is sufficiently detailed.

## Sourcing and accuracy

Before an episode, define the exact mechanism and variant. A generic illustration must not silently stand in for every product using a similar exterior.

Prefer manufacturer service diagrams, documented technical models, patents with relevant embodiment details, educational engineering references, and reputable teardowns. A patent demonstrates a described implementation; it does not establish that every marketed object uses it. A teardown establishes the inspected model, not an entire product category.

Trace each important causal statement, part relationship, and operating state to evidence. Cross-check hidden geometry and motion against at least one credible technical source; use a second independent source for ambiguous or contested behavior. Clearly separate creative inspiration from supporting evidence.

Identify stylization: not to scale, compressed timing, displaced parts, highlighted fluid, removed shell, exaggerated clearance, or conceptual arrows. Verify that simplification preserves the relationship being taught. Use original diagrams or appropriately licensed assets and record provenance.

Do not reuse the current style-board geometry as an engineering reference. All four studies are explicitly conceptual. If the available evidence cannot establish the internal action, narrow the claim, choose a better-documented object, or keep it as a non-published study.

## Boundaries

Explain operation without turning the format into unsourced repair, electrical, pressure-system, vehicle, or medical advice. Select additional domain checks when a real episode needs them; do not add blanket disclaimers to ordinary educational copy.
