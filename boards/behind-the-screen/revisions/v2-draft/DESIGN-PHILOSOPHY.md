# Tangible Signals

Digital behavior becomes an illustrated world with objects that can be touched, carried, separated and retained. A hand and a device establish a familiar human scale. A distinct token makes an exchange visible; a receiving surface shows where it arrives. Meticulously crafted silhouettes do the work that labels did in the earlier draft. The artwork should feel ready to move before any timeline is authored.

Broad curves and opaque shapes create a tactile 2D language. Deep-ink contours hold the important edges, while hard offset faces describe thickness without pretending to be photorealistic. Painstaking attention to overlap makes a thumb belong in front of a screen and keeps a traveling object distinct from its source. A receiver is an illustrative metaphor with a visible state, never a claim that the internet is a physical conveyor.

Pale lilac gives the illustrations room, white defines a surface, cobalt carries an exchange, teal marks a state, and amber connects the human gesture with the object under inspection. A cobalt stage can support a moment that needs a different emotional weight. Color relationships remain stable while composition changes. Master-level execution comes from the scale and connection of these forms, not from a layer of decorative effects.

Motion is a consequence. Preparation leads into contact, contact initiates a transfer, and the receiver responds after arrival. The hand can compress slightly at the touch; a ribbon can describe an arc; a copy can separate while its source remains still. The camera follows the relationship at the moment it matters. Every change is staged with meticulous care so lively movement strengthens the explanation rather than concealing it.

Words are a small supporting material. A technical distinction can be carried by narration while the image shows the two things behaving differently. Bricolage Grotesque and Instrument Sans remain available for a short anchor or a speech caption, but a large title has no permanent claim on the frame. The subtle thread is the weight behind an effortless click: a small human action sets a hidden world into motion.

## Revision critique

The first draft relied on a large question, small interface diagram, several labels and a repeated caption. Removing the labels alone would leave a thin technical diagram. This revision therefore rebuilds the hero art: original hand/device interaction, physical-looking service receivers, a bounded local wipe and a routed media copy. Each hero contains zero visible words.

The three independent studies share contour, depth and color but vary the staging. The first uses a diagonal gesture and an exchange arc, the second a local wipe on a cobalt stage, and the third a foreground/background route. A four-pose strip explores cause and response in the first study. Those are separately authored planning poses, not an animated proof of timing or continuity.
