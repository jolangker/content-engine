# Editorial framework

Editorial revision 7.

## Audience and scope

Put the viewer in the role of the person building and establishing a working backyard cultivation system. The system itself is the subject. Across the episode, the viewer sees a simple first build become a working pond, rearing setup, or related backyard project as the keeper puts it into use and adds what it needs. Explain biology and mechanics at the point where they shape the build.

| Pillar | The build at the center of the story |
| --- | --- |
| Backyard ponds | Establish a household-size fish, shrimp, or eel pond and show how it gains the features needed for the chosen first operating or harvest result. |
| Hatchery and stock | Build a dedicated hatching, rearing, or shelter system and show how its physical arrangement supports the first use. |
| Feed and harvest | Build a live-feed, maggot, or other backyard cultivation setup and incorporate collection into its development. |
| Related backyard builds | Construct a production setup suited to a yard or comparable space, such as planters, grow beds, racks, hydroponic channels, or small-animal housing. Give its first usable result a visible connection to the build. |

The build can take any form that fits a yard. A dug pit is one option among many: tarp ponds on a frame, drums, buckets and tubs, stacked or hanging containers, pots and raised beds, racks and gutters, towers, cages and hutches all qualify. Choose the form that suits the organism and the site, and vary it across episodes so the series does not read as a run of excavations.

## Select the story before the hook

Pitch the project as something the viewer could name after “bikin … di halaman sendiri”. For each candidate, name the bare starting point, the simplest first version, when the keeper puts it into use, the visible events that change the system, the completed result, and what its first output can sell for or save. Use an empty plot, bare corner, unused container, or unassembled materials when it clearly shows that the setup is still being created. The relevant starter stock, seed, or culture may come from elsewhere. Anchor the build in one yard-scale site and around one focal organism or output.

Test the beginning and ending together. If the opening already has the operating setup and the closing only adds a ramp, accessory or technique, expand the premise to the build of the whole system. A maggot ramp, for example, can be the collection stage of a story that establishes a rearing setup, starts its first batch, and follows it to a demonstrated output. Apply the same test to routes through the system, maintenance, and sorting techniques.

At discovery, write the chain of events as an editorial hypothesis. Confirm the actual constraints and responses during research, then record the selected chain in the episode brief before drafting hook options. Choose a fuller build angle if the premise supports only one isolated task or cannot sustain the runtime through meaningful events.

## Hook pattern

Every hook is one spoken run built from four slots:

> **Kalo kamu {aksi 1}.., {konjungsi temporal} kamu {aksi 2}.., kamu sudah punya {sistem}. Tapi, {masalah}...**

| Slot | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| `Kalo kamu {aksi 1}..` | The first hands-on action on the bare site or empty materials. It is on screen within the first second. |
| `{konjungsi temporal} kamu {aksi 2}..` | A time connector and the action that completes the premature first version. Rotate connectors such as “terus”, “lalu”, “habis itu”, “setelah itu”, “begitu”, “kemudian”, or “besoknya”. |
| `kamu sudah punya {sistem}.` | The name of the thing the cover promises, said while the first version is visibly in use. |
| `Tapi, {masalah}...` | The first observable problem, stated as what the keeper sees. Its cause waits for the next beat. |

The two dots mark a short spoken lift between clauses, and the trailing dots leave the problem open for the next line. Keep the whole hook to one breath of roughly 8 to 12 seconds at 1x: the first sentence, which ends on the system's name, finishes within 10 seconds, and “Tapi” is spoken by 12 seconds. The first sentence spans both actions, so it runs longer than the 5-second production default. It has no channel intro.

Vary what fills the slots. Change the pair of actions, the connector, the build form, and the kind of problem from one episode to the next. Avoid repeating the previous episode's first verb or connector. At the hook checkpoint, offer options that all keep the pattern and differ in those slots.

Illustrative hooks (verify every practical detail in production research):

- “Kalo kamu rakit rangka bambu di sudut halaman.., terus kamu pasang terpal dan isi air.., kamu sudah punya kolam lele. Tapi, besok paginya airnya surut sejengkal...”
- “Kalo kamu lubangi tutup ember.., lalu kamu selipkan gelas berisi bibit kangkung.., kamu sudah punya budikdamber. Tapi, seminggu kemudian airnya mulai bau...”
- “Kalo kamu taruh sisa dapur di bak plastik.., habis itu kamu tebar telur lalat BSF.., kamu sudah punya kandang maggot. Tapi, maggotnya kabur ke mana-mana...”
- “Kalo kamu susun talang di rak kayu.., setelah itu kamu alirkan air dari tandon.., kamu sudah punya kebun selada. Tapi, talang paling ujung malah kering...”
- “Kalo kamu gali lubang di tanah kosong.., begitu kamu isi lumpur dan lepas belut.., kamu sudah punya kolam belut. Tapi, malamnya belutnya hilang satu-satu...”

## Story shape

The script follows one chain: **premature first system → problem → improvement → new problem → improvement again → problem–improvement loop → stable system → monetization → closing**.

1. **Build the premature first system.** The hook carries this beat. Two quick actions produce a first version that works just well enough to be named. Go straight to the assembling, lining, filling, planting, or stocking, and leave out everything a careful builder would add up front. Those parts arrive later as improvements.
2. **A problem appears.** Use shows the first shortfall. The hook's “Tapi, …” states what the keeper sees. Then a cutaway connects it to a specific part, layout, or practice the keeper can change.
3. **Improve it.** The keeper adds or changes one thing and tests it on screen. A line like “Airnya sekarang bertahan.” gives the improvement a visible result.
4. **A new problem appears.** The improved system runs longer or under a new condition (rain, heat, growth, crowding, a first feeding) and that exposes the next shortfall. Prefer a problem the previous improvement made visible or possible.
5. **Improve it again.** Return to the same landmarks, show the new part or practice, and test its effect. Earlier improvements stay legible.
6. **Repeat the problem–improvement loop.** Continue while each round adds a real construction change or operating decision and the runtime allows. Plan at least two full rounds; three or four is typical. Each round is one problem and one improvement, and the problems differ in kind. Keep each problem as serious as the evidence supports.
7. **The system is stable.** Show the system handling a condition that broke an earlier version, or follow one last action through the completed setup, such as controlling a water level or collecting the first output. Say that it now runs steadily. The payoff depends on the improvements the viewer watched being added.
8. **Monetize it.** Once the system is stable, give one scene to what its first output is worth. Show the harvest being weighed or counted beside the finished setup, where it goes (neighbours, a warung, a market or a collector), and a price tag hung on the scale with quantity × local price ≈ gross value. When the output is mainly used at home, such as maggots fed to the episode's own fish, show what it saves instead of what it sells for. Keep it to one scene of about 8–12 seconds at 1x, before the closing wide view. Speak rounded numbers and show the exact figures on the tag. A line like "Panen pertama ini kira-kira sepuluh kilo. Di pengepul sekitar dua puluh lima ribu per kilo, jadi kurang lebih dua ratus lima puluh ribu." gives the estimate without promising anything. See [income figures](#income-figures) for sourcing.
9. **Close.** Return to the wide view, show the setup doing what the episode set out to do, and hold long enough to see the result. Close the narration with two beats in order. First, congratulate the viewer and name the result shown, using “Selamat, kamu sudah punya…” or a natural equivalent. Then weave the account name naturally into the spoken next-build question by keeping the exact phrase **bikin di halaman** consecutive—such as “Habis ini, apa lagi yang mau kamu bikin di halaman?” or “Menurut kamu, habis ini enaknya apa lagi yang kita bikin di halaman?”. Omit spoken “follow” prompts; the configured handle lives in [ACCOUNTS.json](ACCOUNTS.json). Tie the congratulation to the demonstrated result.

Use conversational Indonesian with “kamu”, short clauses and concrete nouns. A line such as “Airnya sekarang bertahan. Tapi…” gives the previous change a visible result and lets a new constraint move the story forward. Use a mechanism detail at the moment it explains the keeper's decision. Give supporting components screen space and keep spoken detail on the cause, action, and result.

## Duration and event density

The episode is authored and reviewed as a 1x composition of **90 to 112.5 seconds**, including the income scene, the spoken sign-off, the next-build question and the end card. It is delivered at **1.25x** speed with pitch kept, so the delivered file runs 72 to 90 seconds and still fits a 90-second Facebook Reel. Set this window in the episode brief, estimate speaking and action time at script review, reconcile it with measured narration during assembly, and verify the 1x composition and the 1.25x export at the requested video endpoint. Listen to the delivered file once: the faster speech must stay easy to follow. Map roughly eight to ten meaningful events, the income scene included, as a planning starting point. Use the added time to develop the system through visible construction changes, operating checks, elapsed conditions, and consequences. Keep the hook compact so the premature first version is already in use when “Tapi, …” arrives. The event count is flexible when the project earns the runtime.

### Review the narration

Read the voiceover by itself before script review. Check that the hook fills all four slots of the [hook pattern](#hook-pattern), that every improvement answers a problem shown just before it, that each later problem is a new one, and that the system is called stable only after it is shown coping. Then read it with the visual cues. For each new feature, point to the event that motivates it and what the viewer can see when it is tested. Check that the estimated 1x cut falls within the 90–112.5 second window with meaningful action, includes the income scene, and ends with both spoken lines. Rewrite any beat that introduces a finished component without depicting the action that brings it into the build.

## Evidence and practical interpretation

### Income figures

The income scene states a gross estimate, not a profit or a promise. Base the quantity on the verified yield for the scale shown in the episode. Take the price from a dated source for the same product and sale route in Indonesia, such as a government price panel, a regional fisheries or agriculture office, or a market survey. Record the source, month and location in `SOURCES.md`. Use a reported price range when sources disagree, and say which end you used. On the tag, label the figure as `estimasi` with the sale route and month. Show costs or net income only when they are sourced too. Leave out time to break even, monthly income and phrases like "pasti untung" or "cuan terus". When no price source for the product can be found, show the sale route and quantity without a rupiah figure, and record that gap in the episode.


For production, identify the species and life stage, cultivation setup, and conditions behind any practical claim. Check recommended actions, quantities, timing, expected outcomes, and animal-welfare implications against relevant aquaculture or cultivation guidance. Favor source material that matches the system and locality. Explain which conditions change the result. When an episode can support a mechanism more clearly than a recommendation, show the mechanism and narrow the claim.

Show the scale and sequence necessary to understand causality. Mark conceptual, exaggerated, magnified, or time-compressed views in the production notes. Distinguish an illustrative process from a tested result. Verify any promised yield or harvest interval before drafting the cover.

## Worked story example

**Bikin kolam lele di halaman.** This is an illustrative editorial outline. Production research establishes the practical details for the selected site and setup.

| Estimated cut | Stage | What the viewer sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 00:00–00:11 | Premature first system (hook) | “Kalo kamu rakit rangka bambu di sudut halaman.., terus kamu pasang terpal dan isi air.., kamu sudah punya kolam lele. Tapi, besok paginya airnya surut sejengkal...” The frame goes up, the liner goes in, water and fish follow, and the waterline is already lower. |
| 00:11–00:23 | Problem | In section, the viewer traces the loss to a fold in the liner pressed against a sharp end of the frame. |
| 00:23–00:34 | Improvement | The keeper pads the frame and resets the liner. An elapsed-time cut shows the waterline holding. |
| 00:34–00:46 | New problem | A downpour fills the working pond further. The view holds on the narrowing space at the rim. |
| 00:46–00:59 | Improvement again | The keeper adds an overflow outlet. The viewer follows water away from the rim and sees where it lands. |
| 00:59–01:12 | Loop: problem | A puddle from the outlet reaches the footpath. The water needs a different destination. |
| 01:12–01:22 | Loop: improvement | The keeper connects a receiving route to a planted bed. |
| 01:22–01:30 | Stable system | At a later rain, the pond, the path and the bed are compared in one continuous shot. The level holds and the path stays dry. |
| 01:30–01:40 | Monetization | Beside the pond, the first catfish go on a hanging scale. A price tag on the scale reads quantity × collector price ≈ gross value, marked as an estimate. |
| 01:40–01:50 | Closing | Return to the wide view of the operating pond. “Selamat, kamu sudah punya kolam lele sendiri, lengkap dengan jalur untuk air hujan. Habis ini, apa lagi yang mau kamu bikin di halaman?” The film ends on the thumbnail end card. |

At 1.25x this 110-second outline is delivered in 88 seconds.

### Sample script

**Bikin kolam lele dalam ember.** A full illustrative narration in the v7 pattern for a build with no digging. It runs four problem–improvement rounds. Every practical detail, quantity and price here is a placeholder until production research confirms it.

| Stage | Narration |
| --- | --- |

## Content package

Write one package and use it unchanged for every account in [ACCOUNTS.json](ACCOUNTS.json). Do not write separate versions per platform or account.

- **Title.** One recommended title and two alternatives, at most 100 characters, with no `<` or `>`. The recommended title is YouTube's title and the first line of the shared caption.
- **Caption.** The title line, then a short description of what was built and its main changes, any disclosures the episode needs, and the closing question. Keep it under 2,200 characters.
- **Hashtags.** Five at most, at the end of the caption, always including `#bikindihalaman`.
- **Keywords.** One comma-separated list for search and for any platform with a tag field.
- **Thumbnail.** One PNG built to the [thumbnail spec](frame.md#thumbnail-and-end-card). Its headline is 3 to 7 words, makes the same promise as the title and uses yellow for the promise. The same image closes the film as its end card.
