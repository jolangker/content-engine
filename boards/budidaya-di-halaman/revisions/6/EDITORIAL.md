# Editorial framework

Editorial revision 6.

## Audience and scope

Put the viewer in the role of the person building and establishing a working backyard cultivation system. The system itself is the subject. Across the episode, the viewer sees a simple first build become a working pond, rearing setup, or related backyard project as the keeper puts it into use and adds what it needs. Explain biology and mechanics at the point where they shape the build.

| Pillar | The build at the center of the story |
| --- | --- |
| Backyard ponds | Establish a household-size fish, shrimp, or eel pond and show how it gains the features needed for the chosen first operating or harvest result. |
| Hatchery and stock | Build a dedicated hatching, rearing, or shelter system and show how its physical arrangement supports the first use. |
| Feed and harvest | Build a live-feed, maggot, or other backyard cultivation setup and incorporate collection into its development. |
| Related backyard builds | Construct a production setup suited to a yard or comparable space. Give its first usable result a visible connection to the build. |

## Select the story before the hook

Pitch the project as something the viewer could name after “bikin … di halaman sendiri”. For each candidate, name the bare starting point, the simplest first version, when the keeper puts it into use, the visible events that change the system, the completed result, and what its first output can sell for or save. Use an empty plot, dug pit, or unused container when it clearly shows that the setup is still being created. The relevant starter stock, seed, or culture may come from elsewhere. Anchor the build in one yard-scale site and around one focal organism or output.

Test the beginning and ending together. If the opening already has the operating setup and the closing only adds a ramp, accessory or technique, expand the premise to the build of the whole system. A maggot ramp, for example, can be the collection stage of a story that establishes a rearing setup, starts its first batch, and follows it to a demonstrated output. Apply the same test to routes through the system, maintenance, and sorting techniques.

At discovery, write the chain of events as an editorial hypothesis. Confirm the actual constraints and responses during research, then record the selected chain in the episode brief before drafting hook options. Choose a fuller build angle if the premise supports only one isolated task or cannot sustain the runtime through meaningful events.

## Story shape

1. **Start building on the first spoken beat.** The cover can preview the result. Open with “Kalau kamu…” and the first hands-on action. Compress the initial build and start of operation into a short sequence. Go straight to the digging, lining, assembling or filling that establishes the first version and sets up its first test.
2. **Let use reveal the next build decision.** Briefly name the first result and show why it seems to work. A line like “Sekarang kamu udah punya kolam lele” makes that early milestone clear. Then give the viewer an observable shortfall before naming its cause. Use the cutaway to connect the cause to a specific part, layout, or practice the keeper can change.
3. **Develop the same system through successive events.** Return to the same landmarks after each intervention. Show the new part or practice, test its effect, then follow a different constraint if it becomes relevant. Previously added features should still be legible. Let the story move through the build, operation, and first usable result without making the conflicts more dramatic than the evidence supports.
4. **Give the system a final job to do.** Follow one last action through the completed setup, such as controlling a water level or collecting the first supported output. Make the payoff depend on features the viewer has seen the keeper build or change.
5. **Turn the first output into money.** Once the system works, give one scene to what its first output is worth. Show the harvest being weighed or counted beside the finished setup, where it goes (neighbours, a warung, a market or a collector), and a price tag hung on the scale with quantity × local price ≈ gross value. When the output is mainly used at home, such as maggots fed to the episode's own fish, show what it saves instead of what it sells for. Keep it to one scene of about 8–12 seconds at 1x, after the final job and before the closing wide view. Speak rounded numbers and show the exact figures on the tag. A line like "Panen pertama ini kira-kira sepuluh kilo. Di pengepul sekitar dua puluh lima ribu per kilo, jadi kurang lebih dua ratus lima puluh ribu." gives the estimate without promising anything. See [income figures](#income-figures) for sourcing.
6. **Show what you built.** Return to the wide view, show the setup doing what the episode set out to do, and hold long enough to see the result. Close the narration with two beats in order. First, congratulate the viewer and name the result shown, using “Selamat, kamu sudah punya…” or a natural equivalent. Then weave the account name naturally into the spoken next-build question by keeping the exact phrase **bikin di halaman** consecutive—such as “Habis ini, apa lagi yang mau kamu bikin di halaman?” or “Menurut kamu, habis ini enaknya apa lagi yang kita bikin di halaman?”. Keep the opening free of channel intros and omit spoken “follow” prompts; the configured handle lives in [ACCOUNTS.json](ACCOUNTS.json). Tie the congratulation to the demonstrated result.

Use conversational Indonesian with “kamu”, short clauses and concrete nouns. A line such as “Airnya sekarang bertahan. Tapi…” gives the previous change a visible result and lets a new constraint move the story forward. Use a mechanism detail at the moment it explains the keeper's decision. Give supporting components screen space and keep spoken detail on the cause, action, and result.

## Duration and event density

The episode is authored and reviewed as a 1x composition of **90 to 112.5 seconds**, including the income scene, the spoken sign-off, the next-build question and the end card. It is delivered at **1.25x** speed with pitch kept, so the delivered file runs 72 to 90 seconds and still fits a 90-second Facebook Reel. Set this window in the episode brief, estimate speaking and action time at script review, reconcile it with measured narration during assembly, and verify the 1x composition and the 1.25x export at the requested video endpoint. Listen to the delivered file once: the faster speech must stay easy to follow. Map roughly eight to ten meaningful events, the income scene included, as a planning starting point. Use the added time to develop the system through visible construction changes, operating checks, elapsed conditions, and consequences. Keep the opening compact so the first version is already doing something when the story gets to its first turn. The event count is flexible when the project earns the runtime.

### Review the narration

Read the voiceover by itself before script review. Check that it starts with a construction action, names what the keeper is establishing, and gives a reason for each major change between the first version and the final system. Then read it with the visual cues. For each new feature, point to the event that motivates it and what the viewer can see when it is tested. Check that the estimated 1x cut falls within the 90–112.5 second window with meaningful action, includes the income scene, and ends with both spoken lines. Rewrite any beat that introduces a finished component without depicting the action that brings it into the build.

## Evidence and practical interpretation

### Income figures

The income scene states a gross estimate, not a profit or a promise. Base the quantity on the verified yield for the scale shown in the episode. Take the price from a dated source for the same product and sale route in Indonesia, such as a government price panel, a regional fisheries or agriculture office, or a market survey. Record the source, month and location in `SOURCES.md`. Use a reported price range when sources disagree, and say which end you used. On the tag, label the figure as `estimasi` with the sale route and month. Show costs or net income only when they are sourced too. Leave out time to break even, monthly income and phrases like "pasti untung" or "cuan terus". When no price source for the product can be found, show the sale route and quantity without a rupiah figure, and record that gap in the episode.


For production, identify the species and life stage, cultivation setup, and conditions behind any practical claim. Check recommended actions, quantities, timing, expected outcomes, and animal-welfare implications against relevant aquaculture or cultivation guidance. Favor source material that matches the system and locality. Explain which conditions change the result. When an episode can support a mechanism more clearly than a recommendation, show the mechanism and narrow the claim.

Show the scale and sequence necessary to understand causality. Mark conceptual, exaggerated, magnified, or time-compressed views in the production notes. Distinguish an illustrative process from a tested result. Verify any promised yield or harvest interval before drafting the cover.

## Worked story example

**Bikin kolam lele di halaman.** This is an illustrative editorial outline. Production research establishes the practical details for the selected site and setup.

| Estimated cut | Event | What the viewer sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 00:00–00:11 | Build and test the first pond | At an empty corner of the yard, the keeper frames the pond, fits the liner, and begins a water test. |
| 00:11–00:23 | The first version fails the test | The waterline drops. In section, the viewer traces the loss to a visible gap at the first fitting. |
| 00:23–00:34 | Fix the connection and put it into use | The keeper corrects the fitting and observes the waterline again. An elapsed-time cut leads to the introduction of the stock. |
| 00:34–00:46 | Rain reveals another constraint | The working pond fills further during a downpour. The view holds on the narrowing space at the rim. |
| 00:46–00:59 | Add a path for excess water | The keeper adds an outlet. The viewer follows water away from the rim and sees where it lands. |
| 00:59–01:12 | The new exit reveals a drainage problem | A puddle reaches the footpath. The keeper sees that the water needs a different destination. |
| 01:12–01:28 | Complete the route and show the test | The keeper connects a receiving route. At a later rain, compare the pond, the path and the new destination in one continuous shot. |
| 01:28–01:38 | Sell the first harvest | Beside the pond, the first catfish go on a hanging scale. A price tag on the scale reads quantity × collector price ≈ gross value, marked as an estimate. |
| 01:38–01:50 | Show what now exists and ask what comes next | Return to the wide view of the operating pond. “Selamat, kamu sudah punya kolam lele sendiri, lengkap dengan jalur untuk air hujan. Habis ini, apa lagi yang mau kamu bikin di halaman?” The film ends on the thumbnail end card. |

At 1.25x this 110-second outline is delivered in 88 seconds.

For the nila waste subject, a marked particle can reveal the cause or prove the retest within a story about the keeper building and putting a backyard setup into use. It gives the comparison a visible focal point.

## Content package

Write one package and use it unchanged for every account in [ACCOUNTS.json](ACCOUNTS.json). Do not write separate versions per platform or account.

- **Title.** One recommended title and two alternatives, at most 100 characters, with no `<` or `>`. The recommended title is YouTube's title and the first line of the shared caption.
- **Caption.** The title line, then a short description of what was built and its main changes, any disclosures the episode needs, and the closing question. Keep it under 2,200 characters.
- **Hashtags.** Five at most, at the end of the caption, always including `#bikindihalaman`.
- **Keywords.** One comma-separated list for search and for any platform with a tag field.
- **Thumbnail.** One PNG built to the [thumbnail spec](frame.md#thumbnail-and-end-card). Its headline is 3 to 7 words, makes the same promise as the title and uses yellow for the promise. The same image closes the film as its end card.
