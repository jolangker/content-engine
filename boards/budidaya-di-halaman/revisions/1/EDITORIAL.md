# Editorial framework

## Audience and scope

Make backyard cultivation legible to an Indonesian-speaking general audience. Leave viewers with a visual explanation of one practical relationship they can retell. Give experienced viewers a recognizable process and show first-time viewers where to look.

| Pillar | Good episode unit |
| --- | --- |
| Backyard ponds | A single lifecycle stage or operating problem in a household-size fish, shrimp, or eel pond. |
| Hatchery and stock | An observable interaction in hatching, early rearing, stock separation, or shelter. |
| Feed and harvest | A working relationship in growing live feed, separating a harvest, or collecting a return flow. |
| Related cultivation | A small-yard growing system with one clearly visible environmental constraint and a result that fits the same frame grammar. |

Anchor the episode in a yard-scale site and follow one focal organism or output. Prefer a problem that visibly changes the system. A biological fact belongs at the moment it explains a visible event or an important choice. Illustrate magnification, passage of time, and simplified flow deliberately.

## Story shape

1. Let the cover promise one recognizable result. In playback, show the first action immediately and give the viewer a simple model of why the plan seems plausible.
2. Show an observable failure before explaining it. Use a second-person hinge like “tapi”, then let “ternyata” name the causal detail the image exposes.
3. Add one repair at a time in the same layout. Pick approximately three meaningful beats in a one-minute episode, with a fourth only if the causal chain still reads easily. Preserve useful changes across beats so the viewer can see the system accumulate.
4. Return to a wider version of the starting view. Show a functioning system, completed task, or harvest only when the episode has earned that result. An optional last line can invite a specific next backyard experiment.

Use conversational Indonesian with “kamu”, short clauses and concrete nouns. In sample phrasing, “Airnya naik sampai bibir kolam” lets the audience look for the waterline. “Makanya, airnya dikasih jalan keluar” introduces the next visual event. Read the phrase aloud and give the image enough time to complete that event. Drop a clause when two effects compete in the same beat.

## Evidence and practical interpretation

For production, identify the species and life stage, cultivation setup, and conditions behind any practical claim. Check recommended actions, quantities, timing, expected outcomes, and animal-welfare implications against relevant aquaculture or cultivation guidance. Favor source material that matches the system and locality. Explain which conditions change the result. When an episode can support a mechanism more clearly than a recommendation, show the mechanism and narrow the claim.

Show the scale and sequence necessary to understand causality. Mark conceptual, exaggerated, magnified, or time-compressed views in the production notes. Distinguish an illustrative process from a tested result. Verify any promised yield or harvest interval before drafting the cover.

## Illustrative studies

The visuals exercise independent teaching tasks and sample copy. Use them to guide framing and phrasing. Episode research establishes the factual parameters and production would supply its own cut sequence.

- **Kolam lele.** Give the viewer a site overview and use a continuous water route to make the concept of overflow legible.
- **Bak pembenihan.** Follow a visible hose into a basin, then isolate air rising from an outlet. Hold the focus until the viewer can see the direction.
- **Panen pakan alami.** Follow the path from a culture container through a sieve into a receiving tray. Show what continues downstream and what stays in the sieve.
