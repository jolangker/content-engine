Topic: Support matching: distinguishing emotional presence from practical solutions.

## Researched Literature and Citations

1. **Optimal Matching Model of Social Support**
   - **Citation**: Cutrona, C. E., & Russell, D. W. (1990). Type of social support and specific stress: Toward a theory of optimal matching. In B. R. Sarason, I. G. Sarason, & G. R. Pierce (Eds.), *Social support: An interactional view* (pp. 319–366). John Wiley & Sons.
   - **Core finding**: Social support is effective when the type of support provided matches the specific characteristics and demands of the stressful situation. When mismatched (e.g. unsolicited advice for an emotionally taxing event), support can be ineffective or counterproductive.
   - **Key distinction**: Emotional support (empathy, listening, acceptance, presence) vs. Instrumental/Informational support (actionable advice, tangible problem-solving, cognitive reframing).

2. **Unsolicited Support and Marital Strain**
   - **Citation**: Brock, R. L., & Lawrence, E. (2009). Too much of a good thing: Underprovision versus overprovision of partner support. *Journal of Family Psychology*, 23(2), 181–192. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015062
   - **Core finding**: Overprovision of unsolicited advice or intrusive problem-solving when emotional support is desired predicts declines in relationship satisfaction. Recipients often perceive unsolicited solutions as invalidating, patronizing, or dismissive of their feelings.

3. **Validation and Comforting Communication**
   - **Reference**: Burleson, B. R. (2003). The experience and effects of emotional support: What the study of everyday emotional helping can tell us about loneliness and health. In *Handbook of Communication and Social Interaction Skills*.
   - **Finding**: High person-centered comforting validates feelings before moving to problem analysis, allowing physiological and emotional self-regulation to occur first.
