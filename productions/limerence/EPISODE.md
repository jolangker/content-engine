## Selected episode intent

User-selected concept C / option 3: **Kenal dia, atau mengisi yang belum lo tahu?** Begin with an invented everyday gesture: someone remembers the protagonist's coffee order. Show how that observed gesture becomes a conclusion about compatibility and an imagined shared future. Explain limerent preoccupation and desire for reciprocation without diagnosing the viewer or judging the authenticity of their feelings.

Message: distinguish the closeness imagined around a person from the closeness actually developed through shared experience. One memorable gesture is not enough to establish compatibility; imagining a future alone is also not enough to conclude limerence.

Narrative angle: a concrete illustrative scene opened into a concept explanation and a specific reflection. The wording does not claim that filling in unknown details causes limerence.

Primary editorial pillar: Facts and interpretations; secondary pillar: Inner experience.

Viewer takeaway: separate what the person actually did from the hopes and conclusions attached to it, notice the effect of persistent preoccupation on everyday life, and ask which parts of the closeness have actually been built together.

Visual concept for the later storyboard: observed actions use solid marks; imagined future connections use differentiated outlines. Keep two abstract people, and distinguish actual distance from the bridge imagined around them. These are confirmed concept ingredients from the selected pitch, not approved layouts or an animated storyboard.

## Research boundaries

- Treat limerence as a descriptive/research construct, not a standalone official medical diagnosis.
- Emphasize involuntary preoccupation and desire for reciprocation; uncertainty is an important described feature, not a proven universal cause.
- Do not diagnose from rereading one message, a crush, unrequited love, or intense feelings alone.
- Do not present love and limerence as a simple genuine/fake binary.
- Do not infer childhood trauma, an attachment style, OCD, addiction, manipulation, or deliberate mixed signals from the illustrative scenario.
- A person's distress does not establish the other person's intentions. Abstract actors must not imply equal responsibility or an actual reciprocal relationship when none is established.
- Avoid universal duration, prevalence, neurochemical explanations, or treatment promises. Recent quantitative evidence focuses on impairing limerence; do not generalize its numerical results to everyone with a crush.
- Any reflection or response example is an editorial prompt, not a validated assessment or treatment.
