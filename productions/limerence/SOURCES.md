# Limerence — evidence ledger for Script v1

Researched: 2026-10-18. Scope: terminology, described features, evidence limits, and the selected angle about imagined versus experienced closeness. Script v1 was checked against the evidence below; its clinical scope is descriptive, not diagnostic or therapeutic.

## Sources inspected

### S1 — Wyant (2021), case report and conceptual background

Brandy E. Wyant. *Treatment of Limerence Using a Cognitive Behavioral Approach: A Case Study*. Journal of Patient Experience, 8. First published 2021-11-23.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/23743735211060812
- Full text inspected: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/23743735211060812
- Alternate archive: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8641115/ (direct browser request encountered a bot challenge).
- The introduction describes intense longing for another person's regard, uncertainty about reciprocation, repetitive interpretation of interactions, rereading messages, and mood changes associated with perceived approval or rejection.
- The clinical material concerns one individual who is also the author. Outcomes are self-reported. It cannot establish population prevalence, a general causal mechanism, or an effective treatment for all people experiencing limerence.
- Claims about absence of validated instruments reflect 2021; do not repeat them as a current fact because newer measurement studies exist.

### S2 — Evans et al. (2026), quantitative and experience-sampling research

Chloe Evans, Sian O. Panton, Will H. Strawson, Eleanor Floyd, Stephen Kellett, and Giulia L. Poerio. *Love, longing and obsession: Features, correlates, comorbidities, and real-time cognitive-affective dynamics of limerence*. Acta Psychologica, 267, 107043, July 2026; online 2026-05-13.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2026.107043
- Publisher abstract and highlights inspected through indexed search: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691826008449
- Bibliographic record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42127691/
- Direct publisher and PubMed page opening was incomplete; indexed publisher content supplied the abstract, not full methods or full results tables.
- The abstract describes limerence on a continuum and studies its impairing form. Study 1 includes 1,647 participants; Study 2 follows 51 participants for seven days using experience sampling.
- It supports discussing persistent, involuntary, intrusive preoccupation and links with negative affect in the studied sample.
- Associations with adverse experiences, attachment insecurity, and obsessive-compulsive traits must not become claims that these universally cause limerence. Do not generalize the experience-sampling thought-frequency statistic to every viewer or person with romantic feelings.
- The authors call for formal diagnostic criteria and evaluation of psychotherapy. The episode will not prescribe a limerence-specific treatment.

### S3 — Cleveland Clinic (2025), clinician-reviewed patient education

*Limerence: The Science of Obsessive Attraction*, 2025-11-12, featuring registered psychotherapist Natacha Duke.

- URL: https://health.clevelandclinic.org/limerence
- Explicitly states that limerence is not an official medical diagnosis; describes intrusive thinking, idealization, checking messages/social media, and possible everyday consequences.
- Useful authoritative patient-education support for diagnostic status and accessible wording. It is not an original experimental study.
- Do not adopt all its claims automatically: neurochemical explanations, universal stage sequences, fixed outcomes, and broad causal claims are beyond this episode's evidence needs.

### S4 — Bradbury, Short, and Bleakley, review

*Limerence, Hidden Obsession, Fixation, and Rumination: A Scoping Review of Human Behaviour*. Journal of Police and Criminal Psychology, 40, 417–426. Published online 2024-04-25; issue June 2025.

- URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11896-024-09674-x
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11896-024-09674-x
- Abstract and bibliographic information inspected. The review characterizes a sparse literature and discusses fixation, rumination, and uncertainty.
- Its forensic/stalking research question is narrower than the episode. Do not imply that people experiencing limerence are necessarily dangerous, stalkers, or likely to commit offences. Do not use its limited pre-2026 literature coverage to ignore S2.

## Exclusions and open checks

- No universal cause, childhood-trauma explanation, diagnostic checklist, duration promise, dopamine/serotonin mechanism, prevalence estimate, or cure claim is proposed.
- No factual inference about another person's feelings is licensed by an emoji, a message delay, or a short reply.
- No rigid claim that genuine love is always calm or reciprocal, or that strong attraction proves limerence.
- S2 numerical findings are research context, not planned narration. Full methods must be checked if numerical claims or causal interpretation are later requested.
- If treatment guidance or stronger clinical comparison is added, reopen research for that specific claim rather than extrapolating from the single case.
