## S1 — Original experimental article

Gilovich, T., Savitsky, K., & Medvec, V. H. (1998). *The illusion of transparency: Biased assessments of others' ability to read one's emotional states.* Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 75(2), 332-346. DOI: [10.1037/0022-3514.75.2.332](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.75.2.332).

- Primary text inspected: [author-uploaded full text on ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/13555041_The_Illusion_of_Transparency_Biased_Assessments_of_Others%27_Ability_to_Read_One%27s_Emotional_States), uploaded by Thomas Gilovich. Read the article's abstract, introduction, Studies 2a/2b discussion, and general discussion. Do not confuse subsequent recommended-paper excerpts on that page with the article.
- Identity cross-check: [author's publication list](https://www.thomasgilovich.com/papers) and [Northwestern research record](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/1998/the-illusion-of-transparency-biased-assessments-of-others-ability/).
- Finding: participants could overestimate how apparent their internal states were. Tasks involved lying, concealed disgust, and emergency reactions.
- Proposed explanation: estimates remained too anchored in the person's own experience when considering another perspective. Treat this as the authors' supported interpretation, not an exhaustive causal mechanism.
- Boundary: the authors allow accurate estimates in some situations. Their laboratory "partners" were experimental roles, not evidence about romantic partners. A couple's disappointment scenario is an editorial extension.

## S2 — Independent Japanese experimental extension

Kamada, A. (2007). *Occurrence and anchoring effect for the illusion of transparency in Japanese.* The Japanese Journal of Experimental Social Psychology, 46(1), 78-89. DOI: [10.2130/jjesp.46.78](https://doi.org/10.2130/jjesp.46.78).

- Primary publisher page inspected: [J-STAGE article and English abstract](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjesp/46/1/46_1_78/_article/-char/en).
- Three experiments used the concealed-disgust drinking task. The abstract reports the effect in a Japanese sample, including a face-to-face condition; greater internal intensity was associated with greater illusion in the third experiment.
- Limits: abstract-level review, laboratory task, and no Indonesian romantic-couple sample. This supports a bounded extension beyond the original setting; it does not establish universality or the cause of this fictional disagreement.
- Counts for research traceability only: Study 1a n=45, Study 1b n=46, Study 2 n=116. No numerical claims are needed in narration.

## S3 — Spontaneous-expression and concealment experiments

Holder, M. D., & Hawkins, C. (2007). *The Illusion of Transparency: Assessment of Sex Differences in Showing and Hiding Disgust.* Basic and Applied Social Psychology, 29(3), 235-243. DOI: [10.1080/01973530701503101](https://doi.org/10.1080/01973530701503101).

- Access: the publisher's abstract was available in search results for [the article](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01973530701503101); direct opening returned HTTP 403. Full methods/results were not inspected.
- The abstract reports overestimation in two disgust experiments, one with spontaneous expressions and one with attempted concealment. This provides an initial evidence lead for expression visibility without assuming deliberate concealment.
- Limits: use as supporting context only. Do not narrate subgroup differences, effect sizes, general emotion-reading accuracy, or claims about couple communication from this abstract.

## Fictional scene and application boundaries

- One person was looking forward to time together; the shared plan is canceled. They become quieter and think their disappointment must be obvious. What the other person noticed remains unknown.
- Show internal feeling and external behavior as two different views. Any inside/outside visualization is an explanatory metaphor, not measured data, a brain scan, or a literal signal-transmission model.
- Possible application line, to develop after hook selection: "Gua kecewa rencana kita batal. Gua tadi udah nungguin waktu bareng."
- Naming a feeling offers information; it does not guarantee agreement, an apology, or changed behavior. The episode must not imply that the speaker alone is responsible for the interaction or that repeated disregard after clear communication is explained away by this concept.
- Keep exactly one concept. Do not conflate this with the spotlight effect, attachment style, mind-reading as a clinical distortion, alexithymia, gaslighting, or a claim that close relationships necessarily intensify the bias.
