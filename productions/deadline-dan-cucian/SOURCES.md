# Evidence and illustrative boundaries

## Sources

1. [Falconier & Kuhn (2019), Dyadic Coping in Couples: A Conceptual Integration and a Review of the Empirical Literature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6443825/). Reviewed the systemic-transactional model, stress communication, supportive/delegated/common coping, and contextual factors. Defines stress signals being communicated, perceived, interpreted and responded to. Supportive coping includes problem- and emotion-focused support; delegated coping includes taking over some responsibilities. This review is used for conceptual scope, not an episode efficacy claim.
2. [Pagani et al. (2019), Explicit Stress Communication Facilitates Perceived Responsiveness in Dyadic Coping](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6400868/). Original daily-diary study of 55 married couples. Read abstract, communication process and limitations. Explicit communication was associated with perceived responsiveness in coping; it was not directly associated with relationship satisfaction. Authors caution that correlational data permit no strong causal interpretation and the convenience sample was mostly relatively satisfied/non-distressed. No causal result or universal relationship benefit is narrated.

## Visual limits

Laptop/calendar represent work pressure; basket/clothes/hangers represent actual chores. Connection traces represent communicating, attending and agreeing, not physiological mechanisms or causal improvement. Different actor shapes preserve identity without gender or blame coding. Neither scale nor color diagnoses anyone. A remaining laptop/deadline stays visible after task assistance, preserving the narrow outcome.
