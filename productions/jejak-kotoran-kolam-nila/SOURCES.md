# Research notes

## Scope

An illustrative recirculating backyard tank for grow-out Nile tilapia (*Oreochromis niloticus*). Follow one representative settleable fecal particle near the tank outlet. The story explains a physical separation step under suitable flow conditions. It makes no design-capacity, capture-efficiency, animal-health, or harvest claim.

## Presentation limits

- Give the tank and chamber visibly plausible water levels for gravity flow. Keep the pump after the settling chamber and downstream filter in the full-system view. The pump moves water around the loop. The settling chamber is where the tracked particle comes to rest.
- Use magnification or time compression for a readable descent. Preserve the difference between a flow cue and a solid particle. Settling depends on particle properties and flow conditions, so the example stands for one settleable particle under the conditions illustrated.
- If the ending shows sludge removal, use the lower collection outlet to explain where the captured solids can be taken from. Treat the outlet as part of the schematic and omit operating schedules, dimensions, or plumbing specifications.
- Keep the downstream treatment visible in the wide view so the sedimentation step remains one part of the system. Use the same water palette across the mechanism and avoid visual signals of purification or a performance guarantee.
