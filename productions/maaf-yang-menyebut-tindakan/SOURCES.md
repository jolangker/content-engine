# Research and claim boundaries

Research date: 2026-10-29. Jina search_web discovery followed by read_url source-body reads. No popularity or trend claim. Fictional scene and board creative references are separate from evidence.

S1. Lewicki, R. J., Polin, B., & Lount, R. B. (2016), *An Exploration of the Structure of Effective Apologies*, Negotiation and Conflict Management Research, 9, 177–196. Original journal page and abstract: https://ncmr.lps.library.cmu.edu/article/id/264 . Two studies compared structural apology components and judged their effectiveness, credibility and adequacy in trust-violation scenarios. The abstract supports the general component-based framing and differences in perceived effectiveness, not outcomes in intimate relationships.

S2. The Ohio State University (2016), *The 6 elements of an effective apology, according to science*: https://news.osu.edu/the-6-elements-of-an-effective-apology-according-to-science/ . Original institutional report quoting lead author Lewicki, read in full. Across the experiments, acknowledging responsibility was rated especially important and offering repair followed it. The six components also include regret, explanation, repentance and requesting forgiveness. Participants read a job-applicant/tax-return scenario; they did not receive a live apology from a romantic partner. We use no numerical effect, ranking generalization to couples, or claim of proven dialogue efficacy.
