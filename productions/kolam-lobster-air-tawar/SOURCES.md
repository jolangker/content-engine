# Sources and Claim Map — Kolam Lobster Air Tawar

Research resolves the biological, behavioral, and husbandry mechanics needed to illustrate a small-scale backyard lined pond/tank (*kolam terpal halaman*) for Australian redclaw crayfish (*Cherax quadricarinatus*, known in Indonesia as *lobster air tawar* / *red claw*). Supporting evidence is separated from illustrative staging.

## Primary and Extension Sources

1. **S1 — SRAC Publication No. 244 (Auburn University / Southern Regional Aquaculture Center)**
   - Citation: Masser, M. P., & Rouse, D. B. (1997). *Australian Red Claw Crayfish*. SRAC Publication No. 244.
   - URL: https://extension.rwfm.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2013/09/SRAC-Publication-No.-244-Australian-Red-Claw-Crayfish.pdf
   - Key verified findings:
     - *Cherax quadricarinatus* is a tropical freshwater crayfish native to northern Australia that is non-burrowing (does not excavate deep dam-damaging burrows, only shallow depressions) and grows best in warm water (~75–85 °F / ~24–29 °C).
     - **Climbing and escape:** Redclaw are adept climbers that escape from tanks when the water level sits near the top rim or when equipment such as airline tubing or cords hangs over the tank walls; suspending equipment from overhead away from tank sides and maintaining freeboard/covers reduces escapes. Crayfish also attempt to migrate out of ponds/tanks if water quality becomes poor.
     - **Light and activity:** Redclaw are bottom-dwellers that are most active in the evening (roughly 6 p.m. to midnight); low light intensity, dark-colored tanks, and partial covers reduce stress from bright light and overhead movement.
     - **Feeding, bottom oxygen, and siphoning:** They accept sinking formulated pellets and plant/animal detritus. Because they live on the bottom, dissolved oxygen must be maintained at the floor interface (recommended >= 5.0 mg/L in tanks/hatcheries; pond floor kept > 3 mg/L with aeration). Overfeeding quickly degrades water quality; excess uneaten feed and wastes on tank floors should be removed by siphoning/draining and flushing with clean water.

2. **S2 — Queensland Government (Business Queensland) Aquaculture Guide**
   - Citation: State of Queensland, Department of Primary Industries (reviewed Aug 2025). *Redclaw crayfish aquaculture*.
   - URL: https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/fisheries/aquaculture/species/redclaw-crayfish
   - Key verified findings:
     - Optimal growth occurs at 26–29 °C (ideal site > 23 °C; mortality at hot extremes of 34–35 °C); ideal pH is 7.0–8.5 with calcium/hardness >= 50 ppm to support shell hardening after molting.
     - Dissolved oxygen should be kept above 5 ppm; if DO drops below 1 ppm, crayfish move to the edges and under extreme hypoxia migrate out of the water over land.
     - **Molting and shelter:** Like all crustaceans, redclaw molt (shed their shell) to grow. Immediately after molting they are soft-shelled and vulnerable to predation/cannibalism by pondmates. Providing abundant shelter—such as short lengths of pipe or mesh bundles—increases survival and growth potential.
     - **Grow-out timing and harvesting:** Stocking uses size-graded juveniles (~5–10 g); grow-out to market grades (35 g to >100 g) takes roughly 6–9 months in ponds, with harvesting via drain harvesting, bait trapping, or flow trapping.

3. **S3 — Northern Territory Government Fishnote No. 32**
   - Citation: Ruscoe, I. (2002 / ©2006). *Redclaw Crayfish Aquaculture (Cherax quadricarinatus)*. Fishnote No. 32, Department of Primary Industry, Fisheries and Mines, Darwin.
   - URL: https://www.aquaculturecouncilwa.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/Redclaw_Crayfish_FN32.pdf
   - Key verified findings:
     - Stocking uniform-sized juveniles reduces cannibalism caused by size disparity.
     - **Quantified shelter effect:** Shelter must be provided in abundance because newly molted soft-shelled crayfish are preyed upon by pondmates; trials showed survival rising from 15% with no shelter to 43–75% depending on shelter type (short lengths of pipe, mesh bundles).
     - Smallest marketable size (~30 g) takes at least ~4 months from hatching under optimal pond conditions, and 70–100 g takes up to 12 months.

4. **S4 — FAO Cultured Aquatic Species Information Programme (reproduced on The Fish Site)**
   - Citation: Jones, C. / FAO Fisheries and Aquaculture Department (2011/2012). *Cultured Aquatic Species — Red Claw Crayfish (Cherax quadricarinatus)*.
   - URL: https://thefishsite.com/articles/cultured-aquatic-species-red-claw-crayfish (FAO source sheet: https://www.fao.org/fishery/culturedspecies/cherax_quadricarinatus/en)
   - Key verified findings:
     - Artificial shelters (such as stacks of pipes in grow-out) are essential and should be positioned so water drains freely through and out of them when the pond/tank is drained.
     - Aeration is essential both for oxygen input and to circulate water between the bottom interface and the surface.
     - **Commercial vs. household tank qualification:** FAO notes that commercial-scale profitability relies on earthen ponds (where natural benthic detritus/microbes supplement feed), whereas intensive commercial tank grow-out has not been economically viable at industrial scale. Small tank holding, purging, and hobby/experimental rearing remain distinct from commercial farm claims.

5. **S5 — Experimental Tank Shelter Trial (Universitas 17 Agustus 1945 Banyuwangi / Universitas Airlangga JAFH)**
   - Citation: Raharjo, S. D. R., Saraswati, E., & Yuniartik, M. (2024). Shelter Differences on Growth and Survival of Red Claw Crayfish (*Cherax quadricarinatus*) in Experimental Tanks. *Journal of Aquaculture and Fish Health*, 13(3), 397–406.
   - URL: https://e-journal.unair.ac.id/JAFH/article/view/55759 (DOI: https://doi.org/10.20473/jafh.v13i3.55759)
   - Key verified findings:
     - In controlled tank rearing of *C. quadricarinatus*, cannibalism appears from juvenile stages and especially during molting.
     - Shelter type had a statistically significant effect on survival rate (`p = 0.001`) and biomass weight (`p = 0.001`): PVC (*paralon*) pipe shelters achieved the highest survival rate (92.6%) and biomass compared with no-shelter controls and other tested substrates.

6. **S6 — Indonesian Backyard Tarpaulin Pond Extension Guidance (IPB University Digitani)**
   - Citation: Azzahra, R. / Tani dan Nelayan Center IPB University (2025). *Tips Budidaya Lobster Air Tawar di Kolam Terpal*.
   - URL: https://digitani.ipb.ac.id/tips-budidaya-lobster-air-tawar-di-kolam-terpal/
   - Key verified findings:
     - Shaded locations protected from direct midday sun help keep tarpaulin-pond water cool and stable (~25–29 °C, pH 7–9, DO >= 4 ppm, calcium >= 50 ppm).
     - Clean water should be conditioned/settled before stocking juveniles (acclimated before release), and routine partial water changes plus removal of leftover feed and feces from the pond floor are required to maintain water quality.
