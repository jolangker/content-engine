# Sources and Factual Claims: Akuaponik Pasang Surut di Halaman

## Topic Overview

Backyard flood-and-drain (media bed) aquaponics: establishing a household fish tank and gravel grow bed, resolving continuous overflow and root oxygen deprivation using an automatic bell siphon, incorporating mechanical solids settling to prevent gravel clogging, and cycling clean, oxygenated water back to fish.

## Core Reference Sources

1. **FAO Technical Paper 589 (2014)**
   - *Title*: Small-scale aquaponic food production: integrated fish and plant farming
   - *Authors*: Christopher Somerville, Moti Cohen, Edoardo Pantanella, Austin Stankus, Alessandro Lovatelli
   - *Publisher*: Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO), Rome
   - *Coverage*:
     - Chapter 2: The Aquaponic Cycle and Water Quality (ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, pH, dissolved oxygen).
     - Chapter 4: Media Bed Aquaponic Unit (gravel media volume, flood-and-drain mechanism, drainage cycling).
     - Chapter 5: Mechanical filtration, solids removal, and preventing anaerobic zones in media beds.
     - Section 4.3: Comparison of continuous flow vs. flood-and-drain with bell siphon.

2. **University of Hawaii CTAHR Biotechnology Bulletin BIO-10 (2010)**
   - *Title*: Construction of Automatic Bell Siphons for Backyard Aquaponic Systems
   - *Authors*: Bradley K. Fox, Robert Howerton, Clyde S. Tamaru
   - *Publisher*: College of Tropical Agriculture and Human Resources, University of Hawaii at Manoa
   - *Coverage*:
     - Physical operational mechanics of automatic bell siphons: priming threshold, water discharge volume, break mechanism (snore pipe / air break), and cycle repeatability without moving parts.

3. **Southern Regional Aquaculture Center (SRAC) Publication No. 454 (2006)**
   - *Title*: Recirculating Aquaculture Tank Production Systems: Aquaponics—Integrating Fish and Plant Culture
   - *Authors*: James E. Rakocy, Michael P. Masser, Thomas M. Losordo
   - *Coverage*:
     - Media substrate properties (gravel / volcanic rock), biological nitrification surface area, plant root respiration, and dissolved oxygen recharge into fish tanks via return splash.

## Mapped Factual Claims

### C1: Stacked configuration (kolam ikan di bawah, baki tanaman di atas)
- **Claim**: Placing a vegetable grow bed directly above or elevated beside a small fish pond allows water to be pumped upward and return via gravity, saving yard footprint.
- **Evidence**: FAO Technical Paper 589, Chapter 4, pp. 41–44. Elevated media beds are standard in small-scale backyard units; gravity return avoids requiring a second sump pump.
- **Scope & Limitations**: The support frame must hold substantial weight (wet gravel media weighs ~1.5–1.8 kg/L). Depicted as a stable yard frame or sturdy masonry/container stand.

### C2: Continuous flood risk & root hypoxia (air tergenang nonstop menyebabkan akar busuk)
- **Claim**: Pumping water continuously without drainage causes overflow; using a simple constant-level drain leaves plant roots submerged constantly, reducing oxygen availability to roots and promoting root rot in sensitive crops (like lettuce).
- **Evidence**: FAO Technical Paper 589, pp. 43–45; Rakocy et al. (2006), p. 5. Constant saturation without high dissolved oxygen reduces root respiration and limits nutrient uptake; periodic draining draws air directly into the root zone.
- **Scope & Limitations**: Some aquatic plants (e.g., kangkung / water spinach) tolerate constant immersion, but general vegetables (lettuce, mint, tomatoes) perform significantly better in flood-and-drain cycles.

### C3: Bell siphon mechanism (fase pasang-surut otomatis tanpa listrik tambahan)
- **Claim**: A bell siphon automatically initiates rapid drainage when the water level reaches the inner standpipe crest, drains the bed rapidly, and breaks the siphon when air enters the bottom teeth/snore pipe, restarting the fill cycle without mechanical valves or electronic timers.
- **Evidence**: Fox, Howerton & Tamaru (2010), pp. 1–4; FAO Technical Paper 589, pp. 44–46.
- **Mechanism Details**:
  - *Flood (Pasang)*: Continuous inflow slowly fills gravel pore volume.
  - *Siphon trigger*: Water crests over the inner standpipe, displacing air under the bell cap and creating negative pressure that pulls water through rapidly.
  - *Drain (Surut)*: Outflow velocity exceeds inflow rate, rapidly emptying the bed down to the bell cutouts/snore tube.
  - *Air break*: Atmospheric air enters the bottom cutouts, breaking the vacuum and allowing the bed to refill.

### C4: Oxygenation during drain and return (aerasi ganda)
- **Claim**: During the drain cycle, receding water draws fresh atmospheric air into the gravel bed (aerating nitrifying bacteria and roots), while water discharging back into the fish pond creates bubbles and turbulence that replenish dissolved oxygen for the fish.
- **Evidence**: FAO Technical Paper 589, pp. 21, 45; SRAC Pub 454, p. 6.
- **Scope & Limitations**: While return splash provides significant aeration, supplemental aeration (air pump/diffuser) may still be necessary in hot tropical weather or high fish stocking densities.

### C5: Nitrification in gravel media (bakteri pengurai kotoran jadi pupuk)
- **Claim**: Beneficial nitrifying bacteria (*Nitrosomonas* and *Nitrobacter*) naturally colonize gravel surfaces, converting toxic ammonia excreted by fish into nitrite, and then into nitrate, which plants absorb as primary fertilizer.
- **Evidence**: FAO Technical Paper 589, Chapter 2, pp. 15–18.
- **Scope & Limitations**: Biological conversion requires established bacterial colonies (cycling takes 2–4 weeks). Plants require nitrate and micronutrients; water pH must stay between 6.5 and 7.5 for optimal bacterial activity and plant absorption.

### C6: Solids settling / filtration (pengendapan kotoran padat mencegah sumbatan)
- **Claim**: Heavy, settleable fish feces and feed residues can accumulate in the gravel pores over time, creating anaerobic dead zones and clogging the bed. Adding a simple solids settling chamber (swirl / settling tube) before the grow bed removes dense particles, keeping gravel clean.
- **Evidence**: FAO Technical Paper 589, Chapter 5, pp. 51–56; Chapter 4, p. 43.
- **Scope & Limitations**: A settling chamber removes settleable suspended solids (~60–80% of particulate waste), but requires periodic purging/flushing of accumulated sludge from the bottom drain.

## Verification & Limits

- The scenario is designed as an illustrated educational sequence for a backyard hobbyist, not an engineered commercial farm.
- All numbers, pipe diameters, and flow rates in dialogue/narration are kept descriptive rather than rigidly prescriptive, matching the visual craft rules of `budidaya-di-halaman`.
- No unsubstantiated yields, harvest weights, or instant bacterial cycling times are claimed.
